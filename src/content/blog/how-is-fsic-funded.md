## What kind of funding model does FSIC plan?

FSIC's Master Plan describes "a diversified, sustainable funding model" (p. 25). It says the strategy "balances philanthropy, public funding, employer partnerships, earned revenue, and federal grants."

The plan lists six guiding principles for that model (p. 25):

- Diversified revenue streams
- Conservative budgeting and phased growth
- Strong alignment with funder priorities
- Transparent reporting and accountability
- Early wins to build credibility
- Long-term sustainability through earned revenue

## Which funding streams does the plan describe, and when?

The plan sets out six funding streams, most with the years in which they apply (pp. 25–26):

- **Startup funding:** Years 1–2
- **Public funding and government grants:** Years 1–5
- **Employer partnerships and corporate support:** years not stated
- **Earned revenue:** Years 2–5
- **Federal innovation and research funding:** Years 2–5
- **Long-term sustainability model:** Years 3–5

Each stream is described, with its sources, on the [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#funding) page.

## How is FSIC funded in its first year?

In the Master Plan, mainly by startup funding. The plan's primary startup sources are philanthropic contributions, family foundations, corporate sponsorships, local government support and in-kind contributions of space, equipment and staffing (p. 25). Startup funding "focuses on establishing core operations, launching the Talent Engine, and building early momentum" (p. 26).

The plan's five-year financial outlook describes Year 1 as "primarily philanthropic and local support" (p. 28). Securing "startup philanthropic and local government funding" is a Year 1 milestone (p. 33), and securing "initial philanthropic and public funding" is a Phase 1 activity (p. 13).

The Year 1 startup budget covers staffing, program delivery (micro-credentials, dual enrollment and teacher training), a facility lease and utilities, equipment and technology, marketing, outreach and community engagement, and insurance, legal and compliance costs (p. 27). The plan gives no dollar amounts. As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1.

## Which public funding sources does the plan name?

The plan lists these as potential public funding sources over Years 1–5 (pp. 25–26):

- State workforce development grants
- Florida Department of Education innovation funding
- Space Florida partnerships
- Economic Development Administration (EDA) Tech Hubs
- National Science Foundation (NSF) Engines and STEM education grants
- Department of Defense (DoD) dual-use technology funding
- NASA STEM and innovation grants

STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. The plan describes FSIC as "positioned to compete for state and federal grants for workforce development, innovation, and economic mobility" (p. 26). Its outlook places the "introduction of state and federal funding" in Year 2 (p. 28).

## How do employers contribute?

The plan says employers "benefit directly from FSIC's programs and therefore play a key role in funding operations and program delivery" (p. 26). Its partnership models are (p. 25):

- Annual employer memberships
- Sponsorship of labs, programs and events
- Equipment donations
- Paid internships and apprenticeships
- Co-funded research and innovation projects

The plan gives no years for this stream. It states that employer funding "strengthens alignment, reduces reliance on philanthropy, and ensures programs remain industry-relevant" (p. 26). What employers do beyond funding is described on the [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#employers) page.

## What is earned revenue, and where would it come from?

**Earned revenue** is income an organization receives in return for its services, such as tuition or fees, rather than as a gift or grant. FSIC's plan lists these opportunities for Years 2–5 (pp. 25–26):

- Tuition for micro-credentials and adult training
- Contract training for employers
- Facility rentals for events and conferences
- Innovation challenge sponsorships
- Startup incubation fees
- Certification and compliance training

The plan states that earned revenue "provides predictable, recurring income that supports long-term sustainability and reduces dependence on grants" (p. 26). "Expanded earned revenue streams" is an expected outcome for Year 3 (p. 34).

## What federal innovation funding does the plan expect to pursue?

From Year 2, the plan expects FSIC's Innovation and Market Engines to "position the organization to compete for major federal innovation funding" (p. 26). Its potential streams are Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer (SBIR/STTR) partnerships, dual-use technology development grants, applied research funding from DoD, NASA and NSF, EDA innovation and entrepreneurship grants, and federal workforce development funding (pp. 25–26).

The milestones follow the phases. Securing "state workforce and innovation funding" is a Year 2 milestone, "federal innovation and research funding" a Year 3 milestone, and "multi-year federal and state funding" a Year 5 milestone (pp. 33–34). The [Phased Deployment](/deployment/) page sets out each phase.

## What is FSIC's sustainability goal?

The Master Plan states: "By Year 3, FSIC is positioned to sustain 30–50% of its operating budget through earned revenue and employer partnerships, with grants and philanthropy supporting innovation and growth" (p. 26).

Its long-term sustainability model, for Years 3–5, combines recurring employer memberships, contract training, startup and supplier development programs, federal and state grants, and philanthropic support for innovation and expansion (p. 25). A "sustainable operating model with diversified revenue" is an expected outcome of Phase 5 (p. 14).

## How does the plan manage funding risk?

The plan names four financial risks: overreliance on a single funding source, delays in grant awards or reimbursements, economic downturns affecting philanthropy or employer support, and insufficient earned revenue growth (p. 30). Its mitigation strategies include:

- A diversified funding model
- Conservative budgeting and reserves
- Multi-year commitments from employers and funders
- Early development of earned revenue streams
- Strong grant management and reporting systems

The plan's governance design includes a Finance and Audit Committee and a Development and Partnerships Committee of the board, and a compliance framework with annual audits, grant compliance and reporting, and ethical fundraising policies (p. 17). [How Does FSIC Report Its Finances and Outcomes?](/blog/how-fsic-reports-finances/) covers the planned reports.

## Is the external funding projection FSIC's budget?

No. For the Extended FSIC (FSIC 2.0) scenario, the Master Plan projects **$800 million to $1.56 billion** in external federal, state, private and philanthropic funding to the region over 25 years (pp. 7–8). It describes this as funding "tied to innovation, research, and workforce development," not as FSIC's operating budget. It is an estimate based upon assumptions that may or may not be accurate over time.
