## What is a micro-credential?

A **micro-credential** is a short, focused credential that shows someone has a specific skill. Where a degree covers a whole field, a micro-credential covers one skill an employer needs, and takes weeks or months rather than years.

## What does “stackable” mean?

FSIC’s Master Plan calls its micro-credentials “short, stackable credentials aligned to employer needs.” Stackable means they can be combined: several related credentials build toward broader qualifications.

## Which fields will FSIC’s academies cover?

The Master Plan lists these fields for FSIC’s micro-credential academies:

- Robotics and automation
- Composites and materials
- Avionics fundamentals
- Precision machining
- Additive manufacturing
- Launch operations support

In the plan’s words, “these credentials provide rapid pathways into high-value careers.”

## Who are they for?

- **Students and families:** micro-credentials tied to real jobs, alongside dual-enrollment pathways.
- **Adults and career-switchers:** short, employer-validated training programs, with clear pathways into high-wage technical careers.
- **Employers:** a local talent pipeline. Employers help validate the curriculum and credentials.

More on each audience is on the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#audiences) page.

## When do they start?

Micro-credential academies are part of Phase 1, the first year, when FSIC launches its Talent Engine. The Master Plan’s goal for that phase is the first cohort of students enrolled in micro-credentials. The [Phased Deployment](/deployment/) page sets out each phase.
