## What does “job-year” mean?

A **job-year** is one job held for one year. It measures work in time, not in people: a job that lasts five years counts as five job-years, and five jobs that each last one year also count as five.

## Why count job-years instead of jobs?

A count of job-years lets one figure cover jobs of different lengths over a long period, such as construction work that lasts a few years and operations roles that continue. That is why long-range projections, like the 25 years in FSIC’s Master Plan, are often stated in job-years.

## What does FSIC’s figure cover?

The Master Plan projects **74,500–79,500 job-years** over 25 years with full implementation, across aerospace, engineering, advanced manufacturing, and technology. The jobs span construction, operations, research, training, and the broader supply chain.

The figure belongs to the plan’s **FSIC 2.0** scenario: a fully realized innovation district. The [Economic Impact](/economic-impact/#scenarios) page compares it with the Baseline and FSIC 1.0 scenarios.

## Why is it a range?

Every figure in the analysis is given as a low and a high estimate, and FSIC always shows both. These are estimates based upon assumptions that may or may not be accurate over time.

## How does it relate to the other figures?

The same scenario projects **$1.35–$1.55 billion** in new wages for Florida workers and **$690–$910 million** in fiscal revenue for local and state governments, alongside **$10.2–$12.4 billion** in total economic output. All four figures are on the [Economic Impact](/economic-impact/) page, with their source.
