## Where do these numbers come from?

The figures in this post come from **Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS)**, a survey of employers run by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). It estimates how many people hold each occupation in an area and what they are paid. The latest estimates, used here, are for **May 2025**.

BLS publishes the estimates for metropolitan areas, which are groups of counties defined by the U.S. Office of Management and Budget. Three of them surround Southeast Volusia:

- **Brevard:** the Palm Bay-Melbourne-Titusville area, which is Brevard County, home of Cape Canaveral.
- **Volusia–Flagler:** the Deltona-Daytona Beach-Ormond Beach area, which is Volusia and Flagler counties. Southeast Volusia, including New Smyrna Beach, is in this area.
- **Orlando:** the Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford area, which is Lake, Orange, Osceola and Seminole counties.

## Which jobs does this post cover?

The post covers 12 occupations that match the training fields in the FSIC Master Plan. The Master Plan plans micro-credential academies in robotics and automation, composites and materials, avionics fundamentals, precision machining, additive manufacturing and launch operations support, and dual-enrollment pathways in engineering technology, advanced manufacturing, computer science and cybersecurity, and aerospace technology.

The occupations below are the closest matches in the federal occupation list:

- **Aerospace:** aerospace engineers; aerospace engineering and operations technologists and technicians; aircraft structure, surfaces, rigging and systems assemblers.
- **Avionics and electronics:** avionics technicians; electrical and electronic engineering technologists and technicians.
- **Robotics and automation:** electro-mechanical and mechatronics technologists and technicians; industrial machinery mechanics.
- **Precision machining:** machinists; computer numerically controlled (CNC) tool operators; CNC tool programmers.
- **Advanced manufacturing:** welders, cutters, solderers and brazers.
- **Cybersecurity:** information security analysts.

Composites and additive manufacturing are left out: neither has a federal occupation of its own that matches it closely.

## How should the tables be read?

- **All industries, not only aerospace.** Each figure counts everyone in that occupation in the area, whatever their employer. A welder at a construction company and a welder at an aerospace manufacturer are counted together.
- **Employees only.** The estimates do not include self-employed workers.
- **Annual wages are hourly wages times 2,080 hours.** That is how BLS calculates them.
- **Small numbers are less precise.** Estimates for occupations with few workers in an area carry wider margins of error. BLS reports a relative standard error of 40% for Brevard's industrial machinery mechanics count, for example, and 46.4% for Orlando's CNC tool programmers.

## What do the numbers show?

**Aerospace work is concentrated in Brevard.** Brevard had 1,360 aerospace engineers in May 2025, against 50 in Volusia–Flagler and 240 in the Orlando area. BLS published estimates for aerospace technicians and aircraft assemblers in Brevard only.

**Most of these jobs pay above the area's median.** In Brevard, 11 of the 12 occupations had a median wage above the county's median for all jobs, $49,030; industrial machinery mechanics were the exception. In Volusia–Flagler, all seven occupations with published estimates paid above the area's median for all jobs, $44,940.

**Volusia–Flagler has the manufacturing trades, at a smaller scale.** The area had 400 machinists, 380 welders and 360 industrial machinery mechanics in May 2025.

## How does this connect to FSIC?

The FSIC Master Plan describes the gap these tables show. It finds a "severe and growing shortage of skilled workers" on the Space Coast "across engineering, avionics, machining, robotics, composites, and advanced manufacturing," and lists "growing competition for talent from Brevard and Orlando" among the region's challenges. Without action, it warns, Southeast Volusia "risks becoming a commuter community to Brevard and Orlando." [The Strategic Moment](/strategic-moment/) page sets out that case, and the post on [how the space economy is growing](/blog/how-the-space-economy-is-growing/) covers the demand behind it.

FSIC's **Talent Engine** is the part of FSIC that prepares students and adults for careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, robotics and dual-use technologies. Its planned training fields, listed above, point to the kinds of work in these tables. The [Model](/model/#talent) page describes the Talent Engine, and the [micro-credential explainer](/blog/what-is-a-micro-credential/) covers its short credentials.

These programs are planned. The Master Plan places the launch of the micro-credential academies and dual-enrollment pathways in Phase 1, Year 1. As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1, and course and credential details have not been announced. The tables describe the regional job market, not the results of FSIC training. The FAQ answer on [what FSIC will offer first](/faq/#first-programs) lists the first programs.
