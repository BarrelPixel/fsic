## What is teacher upskilling?

**Teacher upskilling** is professional learning that gives working teachers new knowledge and skills for their classrooms. FSIC's Master Plan pairs the two terms, naming one of its programs "Teacher Upskilling & Professional Development."

## How does the law define professional development?

Federal education law (the Elementary and Secondary Education Act, as amended by the Every Student Succeeds Act) defines **professional development** as activities that give educators "the knowledge and skills necessary to enable students to succeed." It describes them as "sustained (not stand-alone, 1-day, or short term workshops), intensive, collaborative, job-embedded, data-driven, and classroom-focused." They may include training in the use of technology in the classroom.

Florida uses the term **professional learning**. The School Community Professional Learning Act describes it as learning that is aligned to the state's standards, "incorporates active learning; is collaborative; provides models; and is sustained and continuous." Its purposes include preparing students "for continuing education and the workforce." Routine informational meetings do not count.

## Why does it matter for career education?

Florida's Career and Professional Education Act asks each school district to plan, with local workforce development boards, economic development agencies and postsecondary institutions, for local and regional workforce needs. Those plans must include "strategies to ensure instruction by industry-certified faculty" and "strategies to maintain current industry credentials."

## What does FSIC plan for educators?

Teacher upskilling is one of the six core functions of FSIC's **Talent Engine**, the part of FSIC that prepares students and adults for careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, robotics and dual-use technologies. The Master Plan describes training for educators in:

- Emerging technologies
- Industry-aligned curriculum
- Hands-on lab instruction
- STEM pedagogy (methods for teaching science, technology, engineering and mathematics)

In the plan's words, "this expands regional teaching capacity and improves program quality." The full program portfolio is on the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#portfolio) page.

## How do school districts take part?

The Master Plan describes FSIC as partnering with school districts "to expand STEM access, strengthen career pathways, and support teacher development." It gives school districts three roles:

- Co-design dual-enrollment and micro-credential pathways
- Provide student access to challenge labs and innovation programs
- Support teacher upskilling and professional development

For districts, the Master Plan lists teacher upskilling and professional development among FSIC's benefits, alongside "reducing the burden on individual schools to build specialized programs." Its message to educators is: "We expand opportunities for your students and teachers." The Master Plan does not name partner districts, and none have been announced. The partner roles are on the [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#partners) page.

## When does it start?

Beginning teacher upskilling programs is one of the activities of Phase 1, Foundation & Talent Engine Launch, which the Master Plan places in Year 1. One of that phase's expected outcomes is "teacher professional development underway." The Year 1 startup budget includes program delivery for "micro-credentials, dual enrollment, teacher training."

For Year 2, the Master Plan's timeline lists "increase teacher upskilling programs" as a milestone, with "expanded student and teacher participation" as an expected outcome. The phases are set out on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/) page.

## How will FSIC measure it?

"Teacher upskilling participation" is one of the six metrics the Master Plan sets for the Talent Engine. Talent Engine results are reported through quarterly program dashboards, an annual workforce impact report and employer advisory council reviews. The Master Plan's first evaluation principle is to "measure what matters most to employers, educators, funders, and the community."

## What has not been decided yet?

The Master Plan does not say which teachers or subjects the programs will serve, how long they will run, who will teach them, or whether they will count toward a teacher's certification. It does not set a participation target. As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1, and teacher upskilling programs have not been announced. More on the Talent Engine is on the [Model](/model/#talent) page, and the FAQ explains [how educators and others can get involved](/faq/#get-involved).
