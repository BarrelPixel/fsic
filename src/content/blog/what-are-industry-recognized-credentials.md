## What is an industry-recognized credential?

An **industry-recognized credential** is proof that a person has skills an industry has set standards for. Florida's education law gives a precise version of the idea. It defines an **industry certification** as "a voluntary process through which students are assessed by an independent, third-party certifying entity using predetermined standards for knowledge, skills, and competencies," resulting in a credential identified on the state's Master Credentials List.

Two parts of that definition matter most: the assessment is done by an independent, third-party certifying body, and the standards for knowledge, skills and competencies are set in advance.

## What is Florida's Master Credentials List?

The **Master Credentials List** is Florida's list of approved "credentials of value." It is built by a Credentials Review Committee that the state workforce development board appoints. The committee includes the chancellors of Florida's public schools, career and adult education, the Florida College System and the State University System, along with members from local workforce development boards, industry associations and Florida-based businesses.

By law, the credentials of value must include:

- Registered apprenticeship programs
- Industry certifications
- Licenses
- Career certificates, college credit certificates, advanced technical certificates and applied technology diplomas
- Associate, bachelor's and graduate degrees

The committee reviews credential applications at least quarterly and the whole list once a year, and phases out credentials that no longer meet its framework of quality.

## How does Florida decide a credential has value?

For credentials other than degrees, Florida law says the committee's criteria should, at a minimum, require evidence that:

- The credential meets labor market demand, statewide or locally.
- The skills it certifies are aligned with that demand.
- People who earn it have employment and earnings outcomes that provide middle- to high-level wages.

A credential that does not lead to those wages on its own can still qualify if it is part of a sequence of credentials required for a next-level occupation that does.

## What is CAPE?

**CAPE** stands for Career and Professional Education. The Florida Career and Professional Education Act creates "a statewide planning partnership between the business and education communities." Among its purposes are career-themed courses that "lead to industry certification," a response to Florida's critical workforce needs, and access for residents to high-wage and high-demand careers.

Under the act, the State Board of Education adopts, at least once a year, a **CAPE Industry Certification Funding List**. It assigns categories to certifications on the Master Credentials List that meet a statewide, regional or local demand.

## Why do industry-recognized credentials matter?

Florida law gives them weight in several ways:

- **For employers,** credentials reach the Master Credentials List on evidence of labor market demand, which must include employer information on present credential use or emerging opportunities.
- **For students,** a grade in a course at level 3 or above that leads to an industry certification is weighted the same as an honors course when calculating grade point average. Some CAPE industry certifications earned in high school also articulate for college credit.
- **For schools and colleges,** the CAPE Industry Certification Funding List is used to determine annual performance funding for school districts and Florida College System institutions.

## How does FSIC's Master Plan use credentials?

The **Talent Engine** is the part of FSIC that prepares students and adults for careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, robotics and dual-use technologies. The Master Plan describes it as "a coordinated, employer-validated workforce pipeline."

Its programs include **micro-credential academies**, which the Master Plan describes as offering "short, stackable credentials aligned to employer needs." The academies are planned in robotics and automation, composites and materials, avionics fundamentals, precision machining, additive manufacturing and launch operations support. The [micro-credential explainer](/blog/what-is-a-micro-credential/) covers them in more detail.

The Master Plan ties these credentials to employers in three ways:

- **Employers validate them.** One of the roles the Master Plan gives employers is to "validate curriculum and micro-credentials."
- **Advisory councils check alignment.** Forming employer advisory councils is a Phase 1 activity, and the Master Plan names them, alongside continuous labor market analysis, as a way to manage the risk of "misalignment between programs and employer needs."
- **Certification is measured.** The Talent Engine's metrics include the number of students enrolled in micro-credentials, "completion and certification rates," and employer satisfaction and alignment.

School districts also have a role: the Master Plan asks them to co-design dual-enrollment and micro-credential pathways with FSIC. More on the Talent Engine is on the [Model](/model/#talent) page.

## Does "certification" mean the same thing across FSIC?

No. The Master Plan also uses "certification" for its **Market Engine**, which helps local companies enter aerospace and defense supply chains. There, "certification support (AS9100, ITAR, CMMC)" is support for local companies, not a credential for an individual worker.

## What has not been decided yet?

The Master Plan does not use the phrase "industry-recognized," and it does not name the certifications its micro-credential academies will prepare students for. It also does not say whether FSIC's credentials will appear on Florida's Master Credentials List or articulate for college credit. It does commit FSIC to "align metrics with state and federal funding requirements."

Launching the micro-credential academies is a Phase 1 activity, which the Master Plan places in Year 1, and its expected outcomes include "employer-validated curriculum in place." As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1, and course and credential details have not been announced. The first programs are listed in the FAQ answer on [what FSIC will offer first](/faq/#first-programs).
