## What must FSIC make public by law?

FSIC's legal name is Florida Space Innovation Center Inc. Its IRS determination letter, dated October 20, 2026, recognizes it as a 501(c)(3) public charity, effective July 1, 2026.

Under IRS rules, an exempt organization must make two kinds of documents available for public inspection:

- **Its exemption application:** for a 501(c)(3), Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ, "together with supporting documents and any letter or document issued by the IRS concerning the application."
- **Its annual returns:** such as Form 990 or Form 990-EZ, and Form 990-T if it files one, each kept available for three years from the date it is due or filed, whichever is later.

An exempt organization other than a private foundation is not required to disclose the names and addresses of its contributors.

FSIC's determination letter and its application for exemption are available on request, as described on the [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#tax-status) page.

## Which annual return will FSIC file?

Most tax-exempt organizations are required to file an annual return with the IRS. **Form 990** is the IRS "Return of Organization Exempt From Income Tax." According to the IRS, which form an organization files generally depends on its financial activity:

- **Form 990-N** (the e-Postcard): gross receipts normally $50,000 or less
- **Form 990-EZ or Form 990:** gross receipts under $200,000 and total assets under $500,000
- **Form 990:** gross receipts of $200,000 or more, or total assets of $500,000 or more

FSIC's fiscal year runs July 1 to June 30. As of November 2026, FSIC has not filed an annual return. It will post each return on the Governance & Transparency page once filed.

## What does the Master Plan plan for public reporting?

FSIC's Master Plan states that "FSIC is committed to transparent reporting that builds trust with the community, funders, and partners" (p. 31). It lists five public reporting tools (p. 31):

- Annual Impact Report
- Public dashboards on FSIC's website
- Community presentations and exhibitions
- Grant-specific reporting for funders
- Regular updates to local governments and partners

These are planned. FSIC is in its first phase (p. 13), and as of November 2026 none of these reports has been published. What the public dashboards will show has not been announced.

## How does the Master Plan plan to oversee FSIC's finances?

The Master Plan gives FSIC's Board of Directors responsibility for "fiscal oversight and budget approval" (p. 15) and plans a Director of Operations and Finance on the executive team (p. 16). It calls for a Finance and Audit Committee of the board and a compliance framework that includes annual audits, grant compliance and reporting, and ethical fundraising policies (p. 17). "Transparent reporting and accountability" is one of the guiding principles of FSIC's funding model (p. 25).

For the organization itself, the Master Plan tracks financial performance and revenue diversification, and compliance and audit outcomes. Their planned cadence is monthly internal dashboards, quarterly board reports, and an annual audit and compliance review (p. 32). The monthly dashboards are internal, and the quarterly reports go to the board.

## How will FSIC report its program outcomes?

The Master Plan sets metrics and a reporting cadence for each of FSIC's four engines (p. 32). They include quarterly program dashboards and an annual workforce impact report for the Talent Engine, semiannual innovation reports for the Innovation Engine, quarterly business impact dashboards and an annual supplier readiness report for the Market Engine, and an annual civic impact report for the Civic Engine.

The executive team's responsibilities include "reporting outcomes to the board and public" (p. 16). Every metric and report is listed on the [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#metrics) page.
