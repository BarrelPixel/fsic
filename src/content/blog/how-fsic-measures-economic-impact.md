## What will FSIC measure?

The FSIC Master Plan sets out an evaluation framework that "measures outcomes across workforce development, innovation, commercialization, and community engagement." It names key metrics for each of FSIC's four engines and for the organization itself:

- **Talent Engine:** students enrolled in micro-credentials; completion and certification rates; dual-enrollment participation and credit attainment; teacher upskilling participation; internship, apprenticeship, and job placement rates; employer satisfaction and alignment.
- **Innovation Engine:** challenge labs conducted; prototypes developed and tested; university and federal research partnerships; innovation sprint and hackathon participation; dual-use technology projects initiated.
- **Market Engine:** businesses supported; SBIR/STTR submissions and awards; certifications achieved (AS9100, ITAR, CMMC); startup acceleration participation; contract matchmaking outcomes; revenue growth among participating companies.
- **Civic Engine:** attendance at community STEM nights; participation in exhibitions and public events; arts-and-space program engagement; public awareness and perception; volunteer and mentorship participation.
- **Organizational health:** financial performance and revenue diversification; board engagement and governance effectiveness; staff retention and professional development; partnership growth and satisfaction; compliance and audit outcomes.

SBIR and STTR are the federal Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer programs. The [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#metrics) page lists the same metrics.

## What principles guide the evaluation?

The Master Plan describes the framework as "comprehensive, transparent, and aligned with national best practices for workforce and innovation ecosystems." Its guiding principles are to:

- measure what matters most to employers, educators, funders, and the community
- use data to drive continuous improvement
- report outcomes transparently and consistently
- align metrics with state and federal funding requirements
- demonstrate clear return on investment

## How often will FSIC report?

Each area has its own reporting cadence in the Master Plan:

- **Talent Engine:** quarterly program dashboards, an annual workforce impact report, and employer advisory council reviews.
- **Innovation Engine:** semiannual innovation reports, an annual research and development summary, and partner feedback sessions.
- **Market Engine:** quarterly business impact dashboards, an annual supplier readiness report, and federal grant reporting as required.
- **Civic Engine:** quarterly community engagement summaries, an annual civic impact report, and public presentations and exhibitions.
- **Organizational health:** monthly internal dashboards, quarterly board reports, and an annual audit and compliance review.

## How will the public see the results?

The Master Plan lists five public reporting tools: an Annual Impact Report, public dashboards on FSIC's website, community presentations and exhibitions, grant-specific reporting for funders, and regular updates to local governments and partners. Its compliance framework adds annual audits and public reporting, and the board has a standing Finance and Audit Committee.

As of November 2026, these are plans. The Master Plan does not give dates for the first reports.

## How do the metrics relate to the 25-year projections?

They measure different things. The metrics count what FSIC's programs do and achieve: people trained and placed, businesses certified, labs held. The 25-year projections estimate the regional economy as a whole.

For the Extended FSIC (FSIC 2.0) scenario, the Master Plan projects **$10.2–$12.4 billion** in economic output, **74,500–79,500** job-years, **$1.35–$1.55 billion** in new wages and **$690–$910 million** in fiscal revenue over 25 years. These are estimates based upon assumptions that may or may not be accurate over time. The Master Plan does not describe how actual results will be compared with these projections. [What Are FSIC's Three Economic Scenarios?](/blog/what-are-fsics-economic-scenarios/) explains the scenarios, and the [Economic Impact](/economic-impact/) page shows all the figures with their source.

## How does the Master Plan connect program results to the economy?

Its return-on-investment table links each area's outcomes to economic implications. For workforce and talent, more job-ready graduates and higher placement rates in high-value careers are expected to lead to higher regional wages and stronger employer retention. For small businesses and suppliers, more SBIR/STTR submissions and awards and more local companies entering aerospace and defense supply chains are expected to lead to more contract wins and greater economic diversification. The [Economic Impact](/economic-impact/#roi) page shows the full table.
