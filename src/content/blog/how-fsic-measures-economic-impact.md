FSIC will measure economic impact through outcomes that can be verified and tracked: the number of residents who complete training programs and get hired, their starting wages and wage growth, the number of businesses that achieve supplier certifications or win contracts, and fiscal revenue generated for local and state governments. These metrics will be reported annually in public reports and validated where possible through third-party data sources such as wage records, tax filings, and economic modeling.

## What outcomes will FSIC measure?

FSIC's economic impact comes from three sources: workforce development, business support, and innovation. Each has measurable outcomes:

### Workforce development outcomes
- **Program completions**: Number of residents who complete micro-credentials, dual enrollment pathways, apprenticeships, or other training programs
- **Job placements**: Percentage of completers hired in their field within six months
- **Starting wages**: Median starting wage for program graduates, by occupation
- **Wage growth**: Comparison of participants' wages before and after training, tracked over time
- **Employer satisfaction**: Feedback from employers on whether graduates meet their needs

### Business support outcomes
- **Supplier certifications achieved**: Number of businesses that obtain AS9100, ITAR, CMMC, or other required certifications
- **Contracts won**: Dollar value of contracts awarded to businesses FSIC supported
- **SBIR/STTR awards**: Number and dollar value of federal innovation grants won by local businesses
- **Jobs created by supported businesses**: Employment growth in companies FSIC helped certify, train, or connect to supply chains
- **Businesses served**: Number of companies participating in FSIC programs (bootcamps, matchmaking, proposal support)

### Innovation outcomes
- **Challenge lab projects completed**: Number of industry-sponsored R&D projects delivered
- **Prototypes developed**: Technologies tested, validated, or commercialized through FSIC's Innovation Engine
- **Intellectual property**: Patents, licenses, or startup companies resulting from FSIC-supported work
- **Research partnerships**: University collaborations facilitated by FSIC

These are output and outcome metrics—what FSIC produces and what happens as a result. They are specific, measurable, and verifiable.

## How will outcomes be verified?

FSIC will validate outcomes using third-party data sources wherever possible, reducing reliance on self-reported or anecdotal information:

### Workforce outcomes
- **Wage records**: FSIC will partner with Florida's Department of Economic Opportunity (DEO) to access Unemployment Insurance (UI) wage records for program participants (with their consent). This verifies employment, wages, and employer.
- **Credential data**: Training providers (community colleges, workforce boards) report completions to state and federal databases. FSIC will track these records to verify credentials earned.
- **Employer surveys**: Employers complete post-hire surveys rating graduates' preparedness and performance.

### Business outcomes
- **Public contract databases**: Federal and state contract awards are publicly reported on SAM.gov, FPDS (Federal Procurement Data System), and state procurement sites. FSIC will track awards to businesses it supports.
- **Certification registries**: AS9100, ITAR, and CMMC certifications are recorded in public or semi-public databases. FSIC will verify certifications achieved by supported businesses.
- **SBIR/STTR awards**: All SBIR and STTR awards are published on SBIR.gov. FSIC will track awards to Volusia County businesses.

### Economic impact modeling
- **Input-output analysis**: FSIC will use IMPLAN or similar economic modeling software to estimate indirect and induced economic effects (multiplier effects) of direct spending on workforce training, business support, and innovation.
- **Fiscal impact analysis**: Tax revenue generated by wage growth and business activity will be modeled using state and local tax rates applied to verified wage and contract data.

Third-party verification ensures credibility and allows funders, policymakers, and the community to trust the reported outcomes.

## How will FSIC report its impact?

FSIC will publish an annual impact report that includes:

### Quantitative outcomes
- Number of residents trained and hired, by program and occupation
- Median wages before and after training
- Number of businesses served and certifications achieved
- Dollar value of contracts won by supported businesses
- Jobs created by businesses FSIC supported
- Fiscal revenue generated (estimated)

### Qualitative outcomes
- Case studies of program participants: their backgrounds, training paths, and career outcomes
- Testimonials from employers, students, and businesses
- Lessons learned and program improvements

### Financial transparency
- Revenues by source (grants, donations, earned revenue)
- Expenses by category (workforce, innovation, business support, administration)
- Cost per outcome (e.g., cost per job placement, cost per business served)

The annual report will be published on FSIC's website and made available to funders, partners, policymakers, and the public. FSIC will also file Form 990 with the IRS, which discloses financials and governance and is publicly accessible.

## What are leading and lagging indicators?

FSIC will track both leading indicators (early signals of progress) and lagging indicators (final outcomes):

### Leading indicators
- Number of students enrolled in training programs
- Number of businesses participating in bootcamps or matchmaking events
- Number of challenge lab projects in progress
- Employer commitments to hire or sponsor apprentices

Leading indicators help FSIC adjust programs in real time. If enrollment is low, FSIC can improve outreach. If employer engagement is weak, FSIC can refine business support services.

### Lagging indicators
- Job placements six months after program completion
- Wage growth one year and three years post-training
- Contracts awarded to businesses months or years after certification
- Patents and startups emerging from challenge lab projects

Lagging indicators measure ultimate success but take time to materialize. A training program launched in Year 1 may not show full wage-growth impact until Year 3 or beyond.

FSIC will report both types of indicators, giving stakeholders a complete picture of progress and outcomes.

## How will FSIC handle outcomes it cannot control?

FSIC's impact depends on factors beyond its control: economic conditions, federal policy, employer hiring decisions, and participants' choices. FSIC will be transparent about these limitations:

- **Attribution vs. contribution**: FSIC will distinguish between outcomes it directly caused (a resident hired because of FSIC training) and outcomes it contributed to (a business won a contract, and FSIC was one of several resources that helped).
- **External factors**: Reports will acknowledge economic conditions, policy changes, or industry trends that affect outcomes. For example, a downturn in aerospace hiring may reduce job placements even if training quality is high.
- **Comparison groups**: Where possible, FSIC will compare outcomes for participants to similar non-participants (e.g., wage growth for FSIC-trained workers vs. regional averages for the same occupation). This isolates FSIC's effect.

Honest reporting builds trust and helps funders and partners understand what FSIC can and cannot control.

## What is the difference between outputs and outcomes?

- **Outputs** are what FSIC produces: number of people trained, number of businesses served, number of events held. Outputs show activity but not results.
- **Outcomes** are what happens as a result: people get hired, wages increase, businesses win contracts, tax revenue grows. Outcomes show impact.

FSIC will report both, but outcomes are the ultimate measure of success. Training 100 residents is an output. Placing 85 of them in jobs at higher wages is an outcome.

## How will FSIC improve based on outcomes?

Outcome data informs continuous improvement:

- **Low placement rates**: If fewer than expected graduates get hired, FSIC will work with employers to better align training to job requirements, improve career counseling, or expand employer partnerships.
- **Wage stagnation**: If wages do not grow post-training, FSIC will investigate whether participants are underemployed, whether credentials are valued by employers, or whether follow-up training (stackable credentials) is needed.
- **Business outcomes**: If businesses do not win contracts after certification, FSIC will strengthen proposal support, improve matchmaking with prime contractors, or expand technical assistance.

Measurement is not just for funders—it is a tool for making FSIC more effective over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When will FSIC publish its first impact report?

FSIC's first impact report will be published after Year 1 of operations, once programs have launched and early outcomes can be measured. The report will include leading indicators (enrollments, businesses served) and any early lagging indicators (placements, certifications). Full wage-growth and fiscal-impact data will take two to three years to materialize, but FSIC will report interim progress annually.

### Will FSIC's outcomes be audited or independently verified?

FSIC's financial statements will be audited by an independent accounting firm as part of standard nonprofit practice. Outcome data (job placements, wages, contracts) will be verified through third-party data sources (state wage records, public contract databases, certification registries) wherever possible. FSIC may also commission independent evaluations of specific programs or initiatives to assess effectiveness and provide recommendations.

### How does FSIC's measurement compare to other workforce or economic development organizations?

FSIC follows best practices for outcome measurement used by workforce development boards, community colleges, and regional innovation hubs. Metrics such as job placement rates, wage growth, and cost per outcome are standard in the field. Third-party verification using state wage records is increasingly common and considered the gold standard. FSIC's approach is rigorous but not unique—it reflects accountability standards that funders and policymakers expect.

### What happens if outcomes fall short of projections?

FSIC's Master Plan projections (74,500–79,500 job-years, $10.2–$12.4 billion in economic impact over 25 years) are estimates based on full implementation and favorable conditions. Actual outcomes may differ. If outcomes lag, FSIC will diagnose the cause (program quality, external conditions, insufficient funding) and adjust. Transparent reporting allows funders and partners to make informed decisions about continued support. Falling short of long-term projections is not failure if FSIC is delivering measurable value and improving over time.
