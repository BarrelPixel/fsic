## What is an apprenticeship?

An **apprenticeship** is a paid job that includes structured training: the apprentice learns on the job from an experienced mentor and takes related classroom instruction at the same time.

In the United States, a **Registered Apprenticeship** is one that has been approved and validated by the U.S. Department of Labor or a State Apprenticeship Agency. The Department of Labor lists its key elements:

- **Industry led:** programs are vetted and approved by industry, to train apprentices for skilled, in-demand occupations.
- **A paid job:** apprentices earn progressive wages as their skills and productivity increase.
- **Structured on-the-job learning:** training on the job, with instruction from an experienced mentor.
- **Supplemental education:** classroom instruction based on the employer's training needs.
- **A credential:** apprentices earn a portable, nationally recognized credential in their industry.

## Where do apprenticeships fit in FSIC's plans?

Apprenticeships are part of the Talent Engine's **industry-embedded learning**: programs that place students in real industry environments. The Master Plan lists four:

- Job shadowing
- Internships
- Apprenticeships
- Employer-led workshops

In the plan's words, "these experiences strengthen career readiness and employer relationships."

## Who runs them?

Employers. The Master Plan lists hosting "internships, apprenticeships, and job shadowing" among employers' roles, along with validating curriculum and micro-credentials and serving on advisory councils. Its funding model counts paid internships and apprenticeships among employers' contributions. FSIC's part, as a backbone organization, is to coordinate: connecting employers, schools, colleges and job seekers in one regional pipeline.

## Which trades or fields will they cover?

The Master Plan does not yet name specific apprenticeship programs or trades. It sets the Talent Engine's direction: careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, robotics and dual-use technologies. Its micro-credential academies, the Talent Engine's short training programs, cover:

- Robotics and automation
- Composites and materials
- Avionics fundamentals
- Precision machining
- Additive manufacturing
- Launch operations support

Which apprenticeships are offered will depend on the employers that host them. None has been announced yet.

## How will FSIC track results?

"Internship, apprenticeship, and job placement rates" are among the Master Plan's metrics for the Talent Engine, reported through quarterly program dashboards, an annual workforce impact report and employer advisory council reviews. The full list is on the [Governance](/governance#metrics) page.
