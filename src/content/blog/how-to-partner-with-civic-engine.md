## What is the Civic Engine?

The **Civic Engine** is the part of the Florida Space Innovation Center (FSIC) built for the public. The Master Plan calls it "the public's front door into the space economy": the platform that builds community trust, regional identity and broad-based support for innovation-driven growth.

The plan describes five Civic Engine programs: community STEM nights for families and youth, arts-and-space integration, public exhibitions and demonstrations, regional storytelling and branding, and community convenings and placemaking. STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. [What Will the Civic Engine Offer Residents?](/blog/what-the-civic-engine-offers/) describes each one, and the four engines are on the [FSIC Model](/model#civic) page.

## What role does the Master Plan give community groups?

The Master Plan names community organizations, nonprofits and civic groups as one of FSIC's partner types. In its words, they "ensure that FSIC's impact is broad-based and inclusive." Their partnership roles are to:

- support community engagement and outreach
- collaborate on STEM events and exhibitions
- provide access to underserved populations
- strengthen regional storytelling and identity
- support volunteer and mentorship programs

The plan lists the benefits to these partners as stronger community trust, expanded access to opportunities, enhanced civic cohesion and broader public support for innovation-driven growth. Its communications strategy also names partnerships with libraries, nonprofits and civic organizations among the ways FSIC plans to reach residents.

## How do schools take part?

Mostly through FSIC's training programs rather than the Civic Engine. The Master Plan's roles for K–12 school districts are to co-design dual-enrollment and micro-credential pathways, provide student access to challenge labs and innovation programs, and support teacher upskilling and professional development. Those belong to the Talent and Innovation Engines.

The Civic Engine reaches students as well. Its community STEM nights are planned as hands-on events for families and youth, and its public exhibitions are planned to showcase student projects alongside prototypes, research outcomes and community innovations. Each partner type's roles are on the [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#k12) page.

## What about local government?

Cities and counties have a civic role too. Among the roles the Master Plan gives local government is to "promote community engagement and public events." The benefits it lists for local government include enhanced community identity and cohesion.

## When will Civic Engine partnerships begin?

The Civic Engine's main phase is **Phase 4: Civic Engine Expansion (Years 2–4)**. Its activities are to host community STEM nights and exhibitions, integrate arts and space programming, launch regional storytelling and branding initiatives, expand public events and convenings, and strengthen transparent governance and reporting.

Some of it starts earlier. The Master Plan's Year 1 milestones include hosting initial community STEM events, and its first facility is meant to be able to host early community events and STEM nights. Its Year 4 milestones include launching public exhibitions and arts-and-space programs, expanding community STEM nights and events, and increasing volunteer and mentorship programs.

As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1, and Civic Engine program details and dates have not been announced. The phases are on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-4) page.

## How will FSIC measure the results?

The Master Plan's measures for the Civic Engine are attendance at community STEM nights, participation in exhibitions and public events, arts-and-space program engagement, public awareness and perception, and volunteer and mentorship participation. FSIC plans to report them in quarterly community engagement summaries, an annual civic impact report, and public presentations and exhibitions.

## How can an organization start a conversation now?

By email, at info@fsic.space. The [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#community) page sets out what community and civic organizations do with FSIC, and the [Contact](/contact/) page opens an email with the subject filled in for partnerships or for volunteering.

FSIC is also seeking leaders and volunteers with experience across its four engines, including in STEM education, arts and culture, and community engagement. The ways residents can take part are in the FAQ answer [How can the public get involved?](/faq/#get-involved)
