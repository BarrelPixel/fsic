## Why does FSIC involve employers in its training?

The **Talent Engine** is the part of FSIC that prepares students and adults for careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, robotics and dual-use technologies. The Master Plan describes it as "a coordinated, employer-validated workforce pipeline aligned specifically to the needs of launch providers, spacecraft manufacturers, defense contractors, and spaceport operators" (pp. 9–10).

Among the problems the Talent Engine is meant to solve, the plan lists "weak alignment between education and industry" and "limited employer access to local talent" (p. 10). Its partnership strategy puts employers at the center: "Employers are central to FSIC's mission. Their involvement ensures that programs remain aligned with real workforce needs, emerging technologies, and industry standards" (p. 22).

## What roles does the Master Plan give employers?

The plan lists four partnership roles for employers and industry (p. 22):

- Validate curriculum and micro-credentials
- Provide challenge lab problems and innovation projects
- Host internships, apprenticeships and job shadowing
- Participate in advisory councils

The same roles are on the [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#employers) page.

## What is an employer advisory council?

An **advisory council** is a group that gives an organization expert advice without governing it. In FSIC's Master Plan, advisory councils "provide specialized expertise, employer input, and community insight" so that FSIC "remains aligned with industry needs, educational priorities, and community expectations" (p. 16).

The plan names five councils, including an Employer Advisory Council, an Education and Workforce Advisory Council and a Small Business and Supplier Advisory Council. Their functions are to (p. 16):

- Provide technical expertise
- Validate curriculum and program design
- Identify emerging industry needs
- Support employer recruitment and partnerships
- Strengthen community engagement

Forming employer advisory councils is a Phase 1 activity, which the plan places in Year 1 (pp. 13, 33). Its Phase 1 facility plan includes "meeting rooms for employer and advisory council engagement" (p. 18). The councils are described on the [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#structure) page.

## What does it mean for employers to validate curriculum?

To validate curriculum is to confirm that what a program teaches matches the skills the job requires. The Master Plan gives employers that role for FSIC's curriculum and micro-credentials (p. 22). One expected outcome of Phase 1 is "employer-validated curriculum in place" (p. 13).

The plan also uses the councils to manage a specific risk. For "misalignment between programs and employer needs," its mitigation strategies include "employer advisory councils to validate programs," continuous labor market analysis and flexible, modular program design (p. 30).

The plan does not describe the validation process: how often it happens, who takes part or what criteria apply. [What Are Industry-Recognized Credentials?](/blog/what-are-industry-recognized-credentials/) explains how credentials are judged in Florida.

## How do employers take part in the training itself?

Through **industry-embedded learning**: programs that place students in real industry environments. The Master Plan lists four (p. 19):

- Job shadowing
- Internships
- Apprenticeships
- Employer-led workshops

The plan says these experiences "strengthen career readiness and employer relationships" (p. 20). Employers can also bring problems to FSIC's planned challenge labs, where teams work on "real aerospace and advanced manufacturing problems provided by employers, universities, or federal partners" (p. 20). Challenge labs belong to the Innovation Engine, planned for Phase 2 (p. 13).

## Who else helps shape the programs?

Employers are one voice among several. In the Master Plan (pp. 15, 23):

- **Colleges and universities** co-develop curriculum and degree pathways.
- **School districts** co-design dual-enrollment and micro-credential pathways.
- **Economic development and workforce agencies** provide labor market data and analysis.
- **The board of directors** is designed to include industry and employer representatives, alongside education and workforce leaders and independent community leaders.

## How will FSIC know whether training meets employers' needs?

The Talent Engine's planned metrics include "employer satisfaction and alignment," along with internship, apprenticeship and job placement rates (p. 32). Its reporting cadence includes quarterly program dashboards, an annual workforce impact report and "employer advisory council reviews" (p. 32). All of FSIC's metrics are listed on the [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#metrics) page.

## What do employers gain?

The Master Plan lists these strategic benefits for employers (p. 22):

- Stronger talent pipelines
- Reduced hiring friction
- Increased employer retention and expansion
- Enhanced competitiveness for federal funding

Its stakeholder table adds "job-ready talent aligned to industry needs" and "reduced recruitment and onboarding costs" (p. 11). These are the plan's expected benefits, not results.

Employers can also support FSIC financially, through memberships, sponsorships, equipment donations, paid internships and apprenticeships, and co-funded projects (p. 25). [How Will FSIC Be Funded Over Its First Five Years?](/blog/how-is-fsic-funded/) covers the funding model.

## What has been decided so far?

As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1, Foundation & Talent Engine Launch. The Master Plan expects "strong employer engagement" in Year 1 (p. 33), more employer engagement in Phase 2 (p. 13) and long-term employer partnerships formalized in Year 5 (p. 34).

The plan does not say how many employers will serve on the councils, how members will be chosen or how often the councils will meet. FSIC has not named any employer as a partner. The programs planned first are listed in the FAQ answer on [what FSIC will offer first](/faq/#first-programs), and more on the Talent Engine is on the [Model](/model/#talent) page.
