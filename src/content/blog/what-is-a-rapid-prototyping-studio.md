## What is a rapid prototyping studio?

A **rapid prototyping studio** is a workshop where people make early, physical versions of a design, test them and improve them. FSIC’s Master Plan describes its planned studio as “a hands-on fabrication environment” equipped with:

- 3D printers, which build a part layer by layer from a digital design
- CNC (computer numerical control) machines, which cut or shape material along a path set by a computer program
- Electronics benches
- Materials testing tools

The plan states the studio’s purpose in one line: “This space supports rapid iteration and applied learning.” It does not name equipment models, and it does not set access rules, hours or fees.

## What happens at an innovation sprint?

An **innovation sprint** or **hackathon**, in the Master Plan’s words, is one of the “short, intensive events” that bring together students, engineers, designers and entrepreneurs. The plan says these events “build community, accelerate problem-solving, and generate new ideas.”

The Master Plan does not set the length of a sprint, its themes, prizes or who may enter.

## Where do they fit in FSIC?

The studio and the sprints are two of the core functions of the **Innovation Engine**, one of FSIC’s four engines. The Master Plan calls the Innovation Engine “the region’s first civilian-facing applied innovation hub,” where students, educators, researchers, startups and industry partners collaborate. It says the engine “accelerates prototyping, applied research, and dual-use technology development.”

The Innovation Engine’s planned programs are:

- [Challenge labs](/blog/what-is-a-challenge-lab/), where teams work on real problems provided by employers, universities or federal partners
- The rapid prototyping studio
- Innovation sprints and hackathons
- Applied research partnerships with universities and federal agencies

The full list is on the [FSIC Model](/model/#innovation) page and the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#portfolio) page.

## What gap is the studio meant to fill?

Among the region’s gaps, the Master Plan lists “no shared prototyping or testing facilities,” along with no civilian-facing innovation hub and limited applied research capacity. The studio is part of FSIC’s planned response.

The plan names who would benefit from prototyping access:

- **Employers and industry:** access to applied research and prototyping
- **State colleges and universities:** access to challenge labs and prototyping facilities
- **Local businesses and entrepreneurs:** access to prototyping and testing resources, and prototyping resources as part of startup acceleration

## Who takes part?

The Master Plan gives partners these roles in the Innovation Engine:

- **Employers and industry** provide challenge lab problems and innovation projects.
- **K–12 school districts** provide student access to challenge labs and innovation programs.
- **State colleges and universities** collaborate on applied research and co-locate programs or research teams at FSIC.
- **Federal and state agencies** participate in challenge labs and innovation programs.

Among its planned funding sources, the plan lists equipment donations and sponsorship of labs, programs and events. Terms have not been set, and no partners have been announced for the studio or the sprints.

## When will they start?

Both are part of **Phase 2: Innovation Engine Activation (Years 1–2)**. FSIC is in Phase 1, which launches the Talent Engine. The Master Plan’s activities for Phase 2 include “Launch challenge labs and innovation sprints,” “Establish rapid prototyping studio” and “Host community innovation events.” Its year-by-year roadmap places “Open rapid prototyping studio” and the launch of innovation sprints in Year 2.

The facilities plan describes the spaces for that phase:

- A rapid prototyping studio with fabrication tools
- Challenge labs for student and industry collaboration
- Small research and testing labs
- Expanded multipurpose space for innovation events

It calls for “safety-compliant fabrication and testing areas” and flexible lab configurations. The Phase 1 startup facility is planned with a small prototyping and fabrication area, and the final phase with expanded research and prototyping labs. Each phase is set out on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-2) page.

## How will FSIC report on them?

The Master Plan’s measures for the Innovation Engine include prototypes developed and tested, and participation in innovation sprints and hackathons. They are to be reported in semiannual innovation reports and an annual research and development summary, with partner feedback sessions.
