## Where do these numbers come from?

The figures in this post come from the **American Community Survey (ACS)**, a survey of households run by the U.S. Census Bureau. It asks people where they worked in the week before they answered, how they got there and how long the trip usually took. The estimates used here are the **2020–2024 5-year estimates**: answers collected from 2020 through 2024 and combined, which is the only form in which the Census Bureau publishes these tables for small cities.

In this post, **Southeast Volusia** means three cities: New Smyrna Beach, Edgewater and Oak Hill. The figures are for people who live inside each city's limits. People who live nearby, outside the city limits, are not included. Volusia County and Florida are shown for comparison.

Two points apply to every table:

- **These are estimates.** The survey reaches a sample of households, so each figure has a margin of error. The Census Bureau publishes margins at a 90% confidence level.
- **The shares are calculated from the Census Bureau's counts.** Each percentage is one published count divided by another. The mean travel time is the total minutes traveled divided by the number of workers who traveled, which is how the Census Bureau defines it.

## Where do Southeast Volusia residents work?

Most work in Volusia County. The survey estimates that 24,347 workers aged 16 and over live in the three cities. Of these, 88.3% worked in Volusia County, 10.4% worked in another Florida county and 1.3% worked in another state.

Share of workers aged 16 and over, by where they worked, 2020–2024. Workers who worked from home are counted in the county where they live.

| Where workers live | In the county where they live | In another Florida county | In another state |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| New Smyrna Beach | 87.0% | 11.2% | 1.7% |
| Edgewater | 89.9% | 9.5% | 0.5% |
| Oak Hill | 91.0% | 6.5% | 2.5% |
| Volusia County | 79.5% | 19.3% | 1.2% |
| Florida | 82.1% | 16.8% | 1.2% |

A smaller share of workers leaves the county from these three cities than from Volusia County as a whole, where 19.3% worked in another Florida county.

The survey does not name the other county. It cannot show how many residents work in Brevard County, the Orlando area or elsewhere.

## How many work in the city where they live?

Fewer than half. A worker can stay in Volusia County and still leave their own city for work, for example by traveling from Edgewater to New Smyrna Beach or to Daytona Beach.

Share of workers aged 16 and over who live in each city, 2020–2024.

| Where workers live | Worked in the city where they live | Worked outside it |
|---|---:|---:|
| New Smyrna Beach | 44.1% | 55.9% |
| Edgewater | 30.7% | 69.3% |
| Oak Hill | 26.8% | 73.2% |

Across the three cities, 9,312 of the 24,347 workers, or 38.2%, worked in the city where they live.

## How long is the commute?

**Travel time to work** is the number of minutes it usually took a worker to get from home to work, one way. It covers only workers who did not work from home. The **mean travel time** is the average across those workers.

Workers aged 16 and over, 2020–2024.

| Where workers live | All workers | Workers who traveled to work | Mean travel time (minutes) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| New Smyrna Beach | 13,885 | 10,913 | 29.3 |
| Edgewater | 9,786 | 8,861 | 25.7 |
| Oak Hill | 676 | 574 | 27.1 |
| Volusia County | 253,344 | 216,853 | 27.5 |
| Florida | 10,356,895 | 8,733,746 | 28.0 |

The three cities' means are close to the county's 27.5 minutes and the state's 28.0 minutes. By our calculation with the Census Bureau's formulas, the differences among the three cities are within the margins of error.

Share of workers who traveled to work, by one-way travel time, 2020–2024.

| Where workers live | Under 15 minutes | 15 to 29 minutes | 30 to 59 minutes | 60 minutes or more |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New Smyrna Beach | 24.1% | 36.1% | 27.0% | 12.8% |
| Edgewater | 22.2% | 36.7% | 35.8% | 5.3% |
| Oak Hill | 20.9% | 36.4% | 35.7% | 7.0% |
| Volusia County | 24.5% | 37.6% | 27.5% | 10.4% |
| Florida | 20.2% | 37.0% | 34.0% | 8.7% |

In each city, about six in ten of the workers who traveled reached work in under 30 minutes. New Smyrna Beach had the largest share of long trips: 12.8% of its commuters traveled 60 minutes or more, against 5.3% of Edgewater's.

## How do residents get to work?

Most drive alone. Working from home is the second most common answer in New Smyrna Beach, where it accounts for about one worker in five.

Share of workers aged 16 and over, by how they usually got to work, 2020–2024.

| Where workers live | Drove alone | Carpooled | Worked from home | Other means |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New Smyrna Beach | 67.5% | 8.1% | 21.4% | 3.0% |
| Edgewater | 79.2% | 9.2% | 9.5% | 2.1% |
| Oak Hill | 66.0% | 16.9% | 15.1% | 2.1% |
| Volusia County | 72.7% | 9.3% | 14.4% | 3.6% |
| Florida | 70.2% | 9.2% | 15.7% | 5.0% |

"Other means" is public transportation, walking, bicycle, motorcycle, taxi or ride-hailing, and other ways of traveling, added together. Rows may not add to exactly 100% because of rounding.

## How precise are the figures for Oak Hill?

Less precise than the others, because Oak Hill is small. The table gives the Census Bureau's published counts for Oak Hill and their margins of error. A margin of ±188 on an estimate of 676 means the Census Bureau is 90% confident that the true number is between 488 and 864.

| Oak Hill, 2020–2024 | Estimate | Margin of error |
|---|---:|---:|
| Workers aged 16 and over | 676 | ±188 |
| Workers who traveled to work | 574 | ±173 |
| Worked in another Florida county | 44 | ±33 |
| Worked from home | 102 | ±53 |
| Traveled 60 to 89 minutes | 40 | ±60 |

Read Oak Hill's percentages as rough sizes, not exact values. By our calculation with the Census Bureau's formulas, the margin on Oak Hill's mean travel time of 27.1 minutes is about 13 minutes either way. The counts for New Smyrna Beach and Edgewater are steadier: the estimate of 13,885 workers in New Smyrna Beach has a margin of ±898, and Edgewater's 9,786 has a margin of ±922.

## What do the numbers not show?

- **Which county.** The tables show that 10.4% of the three cities' workers worked in another Florida county, not which one.
- **Distance.** The survey measures minutes, not miles.
- **Jobs located here.** The figures describe workers who live in the three cities. They do not count the jobs located in those cities or the people who travel in to fill them.
- **Change over time.** A 5-year estimate describes the whole period from 2020 through 2024. It is an average over those years, not a reading for 2024.

## How does this connect to FSIC?

The Florida Space Innovation Center (FSIC) Master Plan names commuting as a risk for the region. "Without intervention," it says, "the region risks becoming a commuter community to Brevard and Orlando—absorbing population growth without capturing the high-wage jobs, employer investment, and economic mobility that should accompany it." It also lists "growing competition for talent from Brevard and Orlando" among the region's workforce challenges. [The Strategic Moment](/strategic-moment/) page sets out that case, and the FAQ answer on [why FSIC is needed now](/faq/#why-now) gives the short version.

The survey figures are a baseline for that concern. In 2020–2024, most workers living in New Smyrna Beach, Edgewater and Oak Hill worked in Volusia County, and about one in ten worked in another Florida county. The Master Plan's statement is about what could happen as the region grows. The figures here describe the recent past and do not test it.

FSIC has not projected commute times or where residents will work. The Master Plan describes FSIC as "designed to ensure that the growth of Florida's space economy directly benefits the people who live and work in Volusia County." Its programs are planned and not yet running; as of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1. The post on [what aerospace and manufacturing jobs pay on the Space Coast](/blog/space-coast-aerospace-jobs-and-pay/) shows where that work is concentrated today, and the [Talent Engine](/model/#talent) section of the Model page describes the training FSIC plans.
