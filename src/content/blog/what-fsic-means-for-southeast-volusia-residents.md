## What is FSIC?

The Florida Space Innovation Center (FSIC) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization based in New Smyrna Beach. It is a regional workforce and innovation initiative that works through four engines: Talent, Innovation, Market and Civic. [What Is the Florida Space Innovation Center?](/blog/what-is-the-florida-space-innovation-center/) gives the full answer.

This post is for people who already live in Southeast Volusia: what FSIC plans for them, what it says about growth, and what is not yet known.

## What does FSIC plan to offer residents?

FSIC's [FAQ](/faq/#residents-and-families) states: "FSIC expands opportunity for residents of all ages by making the space economy accessible and visible." It lists what FSIC plans for three groups.

For students and families:

- Hands-on STEM learning and maker experiences
- Dual-enrollment pathways
- Micro-credentials tied to real jobs
- Community STEM and arts events

For adults and career-switchers:

- Short, employer-validated training programs
- Job placement, apprenticeships, and upskilling
- Clear pathways into high-wage technical careers

For the community:

- Higher wages and economic mobility
- A stronger regional identity tied to innovation
- More opportunities for youth and families to engage in STEM

STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. Dual enrollment lets a student earn college credit before finishing high school, and a micro-credential is a short, focused credential for a specific skill; they are explained in [What Is Dual Enrollment?](/blog/what-is-dual-enrollment/) and [What Is a Micro-Credential?](/blog/what-is-a-micro-credential/) The programs for each group are on the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#audiences) page.

The lists for students and adults are programs FSIC plans to run. The list for the community is what FSIC aims for. None of it is a promise to any one resident.

## What exists today, and what is still planned?

As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1 of its Master Plan, which covers Year 1. FSIC's FAQ states that it "is a virtual organization located in New Smyrna Beach."

Phase 1 launches the Talent Engine, the part of FSIC that trains people for technical careers. The Master Plan's Year 1 milestones include launching micro-credential academies, beginning dual-enrollment STEM pathways, securing a startup facility and hosting initial community STEM events (p. 33). The public side of FSIC, the [Civic Engine](/model/#civic), expands later, in Years 2–4.

What is not yet known:

- **Dates.** FSIC has not announced when its first programs or events will open.
- **Locations.** FSIC has not announced where programs will be held. A future site in Volusia County "will be determined through a collaborative process with community members, civic leaders, industry partners, and other stakeholders" ([FAQ](/faq/#location)).
- **Enrollment and cost.** FSIC has not announced who can enroll, how, or what programs will cost.

FSIC's FAQ says its website "will include updates, program announcements, and a simple way to express interest in volunteering, advising, or serving in a leadership capacity."

## Will FSIC bring more growth or development?

FSIC's stated position is in its [FAQ answer on developers](/faq/#developers). It says FSIC "recognizes that many residents value slow growth or prefer no additional growth at all." It continues: "Large developments in the region have already been approved, and FSIC does not influence or promote those decisions."

FSIC describes its role as working within growth that is already approved: "to help ensure that what happens within that approved growth reflects community priorities by steering opportunities toward high-wage, future-focused industries rather than low-value or high-impact uses." It adds that "FSIC does not endorse specific projects or drive development."

The same answer names who the work is for. FSIC's focus is "protecting community character while shaping the future economy in ways that benefit the people who already live here." [What Is FSIC's Relationship With Developers?](/blog/fsic-relationship-with-developers/) covers this position in full.

## What does FSIC's Master Plan say could happen without it?

The Master Plan's concern is that the region's population grows while good jobs stay somewhere else. It states: "Without intervention, the region risks becoming a commuter community to Brevard and Orlando—absorbing population growth without capturing the high-wage jobs, employer investment, and economic mobility that should accompany it" (p. 4). A commuter community is a place where people live but travel elsewhere to work.

The plan lists four risks for the region's growth without FSIC (p. 6):

- Becoming a commuter corridor
- Low-wage service-sector concentration
- Limited employer recruitment
- Weak regional identity

This is FSIC's own case for why it exists, set out on the [Strategic Moment](/strategic-moment/) page. FSIC's launch release, from May 25, 2026, puts it in terms of one resident's experience. Erik Ahlgren, who moved home to New Smyrna Beach after working in California's aerospace sector, said: "the overwhelming majority of opportunities in the sector required long commutes to the Cape or Orlando."

## Does FSIC promise jobs or higher wages?

No. FSIC's Master Plan describes aims and projections, and FSIC's FAQ says of its long-term vision that "no outcomes are guaranteed."

The aim is stated in the Master Plan: FSIC is designed "to ensure that the growth of Florida's space economy directly benefits the people who live and work in Volusia County" (p. 7). For residents, the plan expects expanded access to STEM learning, workforce pathways for adults and youth, community events and exhibitions, a shared regional narrative and increased economic mobility (pp. 12–13).

The projections cover 25 years. With full implementation (the Master Plan's Extended FSIC, or FSIC 2.0, scenario), the plan projects 74,500–79,500 job-years and $1.35–$1.55 billion in new wages (p. 7). These are estimates based upon assumptions that may or may not be accurate over time. A job-year is one job held for one year, so the figure counts years of work, not the number of people hired; see [What Is a Job-Year?](/blog/what-is-a-job-year/) The full analysis is on the [Economic Impact](/economic-impact/) page.

## How can residents have a say or take part?

FSIC's FAQ says FSIC "is designed to be a community-powered movement" and lists ways residents can get involved. They include community STEM nights, public exhibitions and arts-and-space events, student programs and camps, volunteer and mentorship opportunities, and employer councils and advisory groups "that help shape training, innovation challenges, and regional strategy" ([FAQ](/faq/#get-involved)). These are planned; FSIC has not announced dates for them.

FSIC's Master Plan also includes a Community and Civic Advisory Council among five advisory councils, which are meant to keep FSIC "aligned with industry needs, educational priorities, and community expectations" (p. 16).

To ask a question or raise a concern now, email info@fsic.space or use the [Contact](/contact/) page. What the Civic Engine plans for the public is covered in [What Will the Civic Engine Offer Residents?](/blog/what-the-civic-engine-offers/)
