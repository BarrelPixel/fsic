## What are SBIR and STTR?

**SBIR** (Small Business Innovation Research) and **STTR** (Small Business Technology Transfer) are federal programs that fund research and development at small businesses. Together they are known as America’s Seed Fund. The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) coordinates them, and federal agencies run their own programs: NASA’s, for example, is one of 11 participating agencies (as of November 2026).

The funding is non-dilutive: a business receives it without giving up equity.

## What is the difference between SBIR and STTR?

STTR awards go to a small business working with a research institution partner, such as a university. SBIR awards go to the small business alone. The rules on how the work is divided between the business and its partner are set out on SBIR.gov and in each solicitation.

## Who can apply?

Small businesses. NASA’s program, for example, is open to small businesses with fewer than 500 employees. SBA also sets ownership and other eligibility rules, listed on [SBIR.gov](https://www.sbir.gov/). A business should confirm it meets them for the solicitation it plans to answer.

## How do the phases work?

Both programs fund work in phases. NASA describes them this way:

- **Phase I** establishes the technical merit and feasibility of an idea. As of November 2026, NASA’s Phase I awards are up to $225,000, over 6 months for SBIR and 13 months for STTR.
- **Phase II** funds the development and demonstration of the technology. Only Phase I awardees may apply. As of November 2026, NASA’s Phase II awards are up to $1,275,000 over 24 months.
- **Phase III** is commercialization, funded from sources other than SBIR and STTR.

Other agencies set their own amounts and periods. The current figures are in each agency’s solicitation.

## How does a business apply?

Each participating agency publishes solicitations: calls for proposals on topics tied to its mission. A business finds a topic that fits its technology, writes a proposal to the solicitation’s instructions, and submits it through the agency’s own system by the deadline. SBIR.gov lists open opportunities across agencies.

## How will FSIC help?

FSIC’s Master Plan identifies limited SBIR/STTR navigation support as one of the barriers that keep local companies out of aerospace and defense supply chains. Its answer is **SBIR/STTR Navigation**, a program of the Market Engine: guidance for small businesses pursuing federal innovation funding, including:

- Topic identification
- Proposal development
- Partner matching
- Post-award support

The program is planned for **Phase 3: Market Engine Deployment (Years 2–3)**; the plan’s roadmap lists “launch SBIR/STTR navigation services” in Year 3. FSIC is now in Phase 1. One of the outcomes the plan expects from Phase 3 is increased SBIR/STTR submissions and awards, and FSIC plans to report the number of submissions and awards as a Market Engine measure.

The Market Engine’s other programs are on the [FSIC Model](/model#market) page; the phases are on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-3) page. For how FSIC works with businesses, see [How does FSIC support local businesses and entrepreneurs?](/faq#businesses-and-entrepreneurs)
