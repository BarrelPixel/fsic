## What role does the Master Plan give colleges and universities?

FSIC's Master Plan names **state colleges and universities** as one of seven types of partner. In its words, "Higher education institutions are essential partners in applied research, talent development, and innovation" (p. 23).

The plan gives them five partnership roles (p. 23):

- Co-develop curriculum and degree pathways
- Provide faculty expertise for challenge labs
- Collaborate on applied research and dual-use technologies
- Co-locate programs or research teams at FSIC
- Pursue joint federal funding opportunities

FSIC is designed as a neutral backbone organization: the plan describes it "coordinating K–12 districts, state colleges, universities, workforce boards, employers, civic partners, and community organizations around a shared economic future" (p. 3). Each partner type's roles are on the [Partnership & Funding](/partnership-funding/#higher-ed) page.

## What would institutions co-develop?

Curriculum and degree pathways. The first of these in the plan is dual enrollment. **Dual enrollment** lets students earn college credit while they are still in school. The Master Plan describes FSIC's dual-enrollment STEM pathways as "partnerships with state colleges and universities" in four fields (p. 19):

- Engineering technology
- Advanced manufacturing
- Computer science and cybersecurity
- Aerospace technology

STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. In these pathways, the plan says, "students earn college credit while gaining hands-on experience" (p. 20). [What Is Dual Enrollment?](/blog/what-is-dual-enrollment/) explains how FSIC plans to expand these pathways.

The plan does not say which degrees or courses the pathways would lead to, or which institutions would award the credit.

## How would faculty take part in challenge labs?

A **challenge lab** is a team project on a real problem. The Master Plan describes teams working "on real aerospace and advanced manufacturing problems provided by employers, universities, or federal partners," with outcomes that include prototypes, research findings and new technologies (p. 20).

Universities appear twice in that description: they can provide problems, and the plan asks them to "provide faculty expertise for challenge labs" (p. 23). The plan does not say how faculty would be assigned or compensated. [What Is a Challenge Lab?](/blog/what-is-a-challenge-lab/) covers the program in more detail.

## What applied research does the plan describe?

**Applied research** is research aimed at solving a practical problem. The Master Plan describes applied research partnerships as "collaborations with universities and federal agencies" in four areas (p. 20):

- Dual-use technology development
- Autonomy and artificial intelligence (AI) research
- Materials and manufacturing innovation
- Space systems and sensors

A **dual-use technology** is one with both civilian and defense uses. The plan states that these partnerships "strengthen regional competitiveness for federal funding" (p. 20).

Applied research partnerships belong to FSIC's Innovation Engine, which the plan describes as a place where "students, educators, researchers, startups, and industry partners collaborate to solve real aerospace and advanced manufacturing challenges" (p. 10). More on the engine is on the [FSIC Model](/model/#innovation) page.

## Why does the plan say the region needs this?

The Master Plan lists "limited applied research capacity" and "weak industry-university collaboration" among the region's gaps (p. 6). It also states that universities, federal agencies and industry partners "are actively seeking regional collaborators, applied research partners, talent pipelines, and community-facing innovation hubs" (p. 6). The needs it lists are:

- Applied research partnerships
- Shared facilities
- Talent development pipelines
- Community engagement platforms
- Dual-use technology testbeds

Its conclusion is that "without a central hub, the region cannot attract or sustain long-term partnerships with universities, federal agencies, and industry leaders" (p. 6).

## What do institutions gain?

The Master Plan says higher education institutions "gain a civilian-facing innovation hub that expands applied research, industry partnerships, and student engagement," and that FSIC "becomes a bridge between academic expertise and real-world industry needs" (p. 12). The key benefits it lists are:

- Applied research partnerships
- Access to challenge labs and prototyping facilities
- Industry-aligned curriculum development
- Student internships and experiential learning
- Opportunities to co-locate programs or faculty

The plan's partnership strategy adds four strategic benefits: expanded research impact, stronger industry alignment, increased competitiveness for grants, and enhanced student engagement and experiential learning (p. 23). These are the plan's stated aims, not results.

## When would the work begin?

Phase 1, Foundation & Talent Engine Launch, is planned for Year 1. Its roadmap lists "begin dual-enrollment STEM pathways" (p. 33), and one of its expected outcomes is "strong early partnerships with K–12, colleges, and employers" (p. 13).

Phase 2, Innovation Engine Activation, is planned for Years 1–2. Its activities include launching challenge labs and to "form university and federal research partnerships." Its expected outcomes include "university partnerships formalized" and "applied research projects underway" (p. 13). The Year 2 roadmap lists "formalize university and federal research partnerships" (p. 33).

As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1, and no program dates or partner institutions have been announced. The phases are on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-2) page.

## Where would partners work?

The plan's facilities are phased with the programs. The spaces planned for Innovation Engine Activation (Years 1–2) include challenge labs, a rapid prototyping studio, small research and testing labs, and "collaboration zones for university and industry partners" (p. 18), with "co-location opportunities for university partners" (p. 19). The spaces planned for Years 3–5 include "co-located university and industry partner suites" (p. 18).

These are planned spaces. The planned programs and facilities are on the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#facilities) page.

## How will FSIC measure the partnerships?

The Master Plan's Innovation Engine metrics are (p. 32):

- Number of challenge labs conducted
- Prototypes developed and tested
- University and federal research partnerships
- Innovation sprint and hackathon participation
- Dual-use technology projects initiated

They are reported through semiannual innovation reports, an annual research and development summary, and partner feedback sessions. "Stronger university partnerships" is one of the plan's intended innovation and research outcomes (p. 29).

The plan also lists an Innovation and Research Advisory Council and an Education and Workforce Advisory Council among FSIC's advisory councils (p. 16). It does not say who will sit on them. The councils are described on the [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#structure) page.

## What does the plan describe beyond 2031?

A formal consortium. The Master Plan's long-term vision, for 2032–2040, states that "by the early 2030s, FSIC evolves into a formal multi-university consortium" (p. 36). The core elements it lists include:

- Multiple universities with a permanent physical presence
- Shared research labs, testing facilities and applied research and development spaces
- Joint faculty appointments and rotating scholar-in-residence programs
- Cross-institutional degree pathways in aerospace, robotics, AI, materials and systems engineering
- A unified governance and funding model

This is a vision, not a commitment, and it names no universities. The site's FAQ says of FSIC's long-term future that "no outcomes are guaranteed" ([What is the long-term vision for FSIC?](/faq/#future)). The vision is set out on the [Long-Term Vision](/long-term-vision/#consortium) page.

## How can an institution start a conversation now?

By email, at info@fsic.space. The [Contact](/contact/) page opens an email with the subject filled in for a partnership inquiry. The ways the public can take part are in the FAQ answer [How can the public get involved?](/faq/#get-involved)

## What has not been decided yet?

The Master Plan does not name partner institutions, set terms or costs for them, or describe agreements on credit, intellectual property or research funding. None of these have been announced.
