## What is dual enrollment?

**Dual enrollment** is the enrollment of a high school or middle school student in a college course that counts toward both high school completion and a career certificate or an associate or bachelor's degree. In Florida, it is set out in state law (section 1007.271, Florida Statutes).

## Who can take dual enrollment courses in Florida?

Students in grades 6 through 12 at a Florida public school, or at an eligible Florida private school, can take dual enrollment courses. Home education students can also take part. Under state law:

- **College credit courses** require a 3.0 unweighted high school grade point average and a demonstrated level of college-level communication and computation skills.
- **Career certificate courses** require a 2.0 unweighted high school grade point average.
- Colleges may add their own requirements, set out in the agreement between the college and the school district.

## What does it cost?

Dual enrollment students are exempt from registration, tuition and laboratory fees, and the instructional materials assigned for their courses must be provided free of charge. Students are responsible for their own transportation.

## Where are the courses taught?

Courses can be taught on the college campus or at the high school, during or after school hours and in the summer. Each school district and its local college agree the details every year in a dual enrollment articulation agreement. Wherever a course is taught, the instructor must meet the qualifications of the college's accrediting body, and courses on a high school campus must meet the same standards as the same course on the college campus.

## How do the credits count?

The college assigns a letter grade, and the school district posts it to the student's high school transcript. Dual enrollment courses come from Florida's statewide course numbering system, and before enrolling, students receive a Department of Education statement on how the course may count toward a college certificate or degree.

## Which STEM pathways does FSIC plan?

STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. FSIC's Master Plan describes **dual-enrollment STEM pathways**: partnerships with state colleges and universities to offer:

- Engineering technology
- Advanced manufacturing
- Computer science and cybersecurity
- Aerospace technology

In the plan's words, "students earn college credit while gaining hands-on experience." These fields match the shortages the Master Plan identifies on the Space Coast, in engineering, avionics, machining, robotics, composites and advanced manufacturing.

## Who does FSIC work with on dual enrollment?

The Master Plan describes FSIC as a backbone organization that coordinates existing institutions rather than replacing them. For dual enrollment, it gives these roles:

- **School districts** co-design dual-enrollment and micro-credential pathways with FSIC.
- **State colleges and universities** co-develop curriculum and degree pathways.
- **Teachers** take part in FSIC's teacher upskilling and professional development programs.

The Master Plan does not name partner institutions, and none have been announced.

## When will FSIC's dual-enrollment pathways start?

Establishing dual-enrollment STEM pathways is one of the activities of Phase 1, Foundation & Talent Engine Launch, which the Master Plan places in Year 1. As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1; courses, partner colleges and enrollment details have not been announced. The [Phased Deployment](/deployment/) page sets out each phase.
