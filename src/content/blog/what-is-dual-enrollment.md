Dual enrollment is a program that lets high school students take college-level courses and earn credits toward both their high school diploma and a college degree at the same time. FSIC's Master Plan describes expanding dual enrollment in STEM fields—science, technology, engineering and mathematics—in partnership with Volusia County Schools and local colleges, starting in Year 1 of the Talent Engine.

## What is dual enrollment?

Dual enrollment allows eligible high school students to enroll in college courses while they are still in high school. Students earn credit toward their high school graduation requirements and college degree simultaneously. The courses are taught by college instructors and are the same courses that traditional college students take.

In Florida, dual enrollment has been available since 2007. Students can take courses on a college campus, at their high school, or online, depending on the course and the agreement between the school district and the college.

## How does dual enrollment work in Florida?

A student enrolled in a Florida public high school can participate in dual enrollment at a Florida public college or state university at no cost for tuition or fees. The state pays for the courses through the Dual Enrollment Program. Students are responsible for their own textbooks and supplies, though financial assistance may be available.

Eligible students typically include those in grades 6–12 who meet the college's admission requirements. Most colleges require a minimum GPA (often 3.0) and qualifying test scores or completion of certain high school courses.

Courses taken through dual enrollment appear on both the high school transcript and the college transcript. The credits are transferable to Florida public colleges and universities, and often to institutions in other states.

## What STEM fields will FSIC focus on?

FSIC's Master Plan emphasizes dual enrollment pathways in fields tied to the space industry and advanced manufacturing, including:

- **Aerospace and aviation technology**: courses in avionics, aerospace materials, and aircraft systems
- **Robotics and automation**: industrial robotics, automation systems, and control systems
- **Precision machining**: computer-aided design (CAD), computer numerical control (CNC) machining, and manufacturing processes
- **Engineering**: calculus, physics, engineering principles, and engineering technology courses
- **Computer science and IT**: programming, cybersecurity, and data analysis

These fields align with the jobs that the Space Coast's growth is creating in Southeast Volusia and the skills local employers need.

## Who is dual enrollment for?

Dual enrollment serves motivated high school students who are ready for college-level work and want to:

- **Get a head start on a college degree** by earning credits before graduating high school
- **Explore career paths** in STEM fields before committing to a major
- **Save time and money** by completing general education or program requirements early
- **Challenge themselves** with rigorous coursework while still in high school

FSIC's focus on STEM dual enrollment is designed for students interested in careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, engineering, or technology. Students do not need to know exactly what career they want; dual enrollment lets them explore while earning credits.

## How will FSIC expand dual enrollment in Southeast Volusia?

The Talent Engine, the first part of FSIC to launch, will work with Volusia County Schools and local colleges to:

- **Increase the number of STEM dual enrollment seats available** to students in Southeast Volusia
- **Add new STEM courses** aligned to the skills employers need, such as avionics and precision machining
- **Provide career counseling** to help students choose pathways that lead to jobs
- **Connect students to employers** through internships, tours, and mentorship programs
- **Support teachers** with professional development in STEM fields

The Master Plan calls for these dual enrollment partnerships to begin in Year 1 of FSIC's phased deployment, starting with existing programs and expanding from there.

## When will FSIC's dual enrollment programs launch?

FSIC's dual enrollment initiatives are planned for Year 1 of the Talent Engine, which begins when the first partnerships and funding are in place. The timeline depends on the pace of partnerships with Volusia County Schools, local colleges, and employers.

Unlike the Innovation Engine or the campus, which require facilities, the Talent Engine can launch sooner because it builds on institutions and programs that already exist in the region. Dual enrollment programs can expand as soon as the partnerships are formalized and additional funding is secured.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can dual enrollment courses be taken online?

Yes. In Florida, many colleges offer dual enrollment courses online, and students can choose online sections when available. FSIC's partnerships may include online courses to expand access for students across Southeast Volusia.

### Do dual enrollment students pay tuition?

No. Florida public high school students enrolled in dual enrollment at a Florida public college or state university do not pay tuition or fees. The state covers the cost through the Dual Enrollment Program. Students are responsible for textbooks, supplies, and transportation.

### What colleges will FSIC partner with for dual enrollment?

FSIC's Master Plan describes partnerships with local colleges and institutions that already serve Volusia County, including community colleges and state colleges. Specific partner institutions will be announced as agreements are finalized.

### Can dual enrollment credits transfer to universities outside Florida?

It depends on the receiving institution. Dual enrollment credits earned at Florida public colleges typically transfer within Florida's public university system. For private or out-of-state universities, students should check with the admissions office about transfer policies. The courses appear on an official college transcript, which can be sent to any institution.
