## What problem does the Talent Engine address?

The **Talent Engine** is the part of FSIC that builds a coordinated, employer-validated workforce pipeline for careers in aerospace, advanced manufacturing, robotics and dual-use technologies. It answers the gap the Master Plan puts first.

The plan describes "a severe and growing shortage of skilled workers across engineering, avionics, machining, robotics, composites, and advanced manufacturing" on the Space Coast, where launch providers, spacecraft manufacturers and defense contractors "are expanding faster than the region can produce talent." It lists the challenges behind that shortage:

- No coordinated, space-specific talent pipeline
- Limited hands-on learning opportunities
- Insufficient alignment between education and industry
- Lack of specialized equipment and training environments
- Growing competition for talent from Brevard and Orlando

## Why does it come first?

The Master Plan calls the Talent Engine "the foundation of FSIC's model and the first engine deployed," and Phase 1 launches it as "the first and most critical component of the model." That phase "focuses on building credibility, demonstrating early wins, and establishing the partnerships required for long-term success."

The order follows from the plan's phased design, which it says "reduces risk, builds early momentum, and aligns with regional capacity." Its first phase needs only a lean, cost-efficient startup facility; the Innovation, Market and Civic Engines, which follow, bring the labs, business resource center and public spaces.

## What does the first phase include?

Phase 1, Foundation & Talent Engine Launch, covers Year 1. Its activities in the Master Plan are:

- Launch micro-credential academies
- Establish dual-enrollment STEM pathways
- Begin teacher upskilling programs
- Deploy the Regional Talent Marketplace, a digital platform that connects students, job seekers, employers and educators
- Form employer advisory councils
- Secure initial philanthropic and public funding
- Establish governance, bylaws and board independence

The phase also establishes a cost-efficient startup facility, with classrooms and training labs for micro-credentials, so that programs can be delivered right away. As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1. The [Phased Deployment](/deployment/) page sets out every phase.

## Who does FSIC work with?

FSIC is designed as a backbone organization: it coordinates existing institutions rather than replacing them. The Master Plan gives each partner group a role in the Talent Engine:

- **Employers** validate curriculum and micro-credentials, host internships, apprenticeships and job shadowing, and sit on advisory councils.
- **School districts** co-design dual-enrollment and micro-credential pathways and support teacher upskilling.
- **State colleges and universities** co-develop curriculum and degree pathways.
- **Economic development and workforce agencies** provide labor market data and collaborate on workforce initiatives.

The Master Plan describes these roles; it does not name partner organizations.

## How will FSIC know it is working?

The Master Plan sets Talent Engine metrics: students enrolled in micro-credentials, completion and certification rates, dual-enrollment participation and credit attainment, teacher upskilling participation, internship, apprenticeship and job placement rates, and employer satisfaction. They are reported through quarterly program dashboards, an annual workforce impact report and employer advisory council reviews. The full list is on the [Governance](/governance#metrics) page.
