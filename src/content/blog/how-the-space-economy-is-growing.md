## How fast is the space economy growing?

The **space economy** is the value created by space hardware and services, and by the many industries that rely on them. The World Economic Forum and McKinsey & Company estimate that the global space economy will grow from **$630 billion in 2023 to $1.8 trillion by 2035**, an average of 9% a year. That is well above the 5% a year they expect for global gross domestic product (GDP) over the same period.

## What is driving the growth?

The report names four main drivers:

- **Lower launch costs.** Launch costs have fallen tenfold over the last 20 years, and the number of satellites launched each year grew by more than 50% a year from 2019 to 2023.
- **Commercial innovation.** Better components and software make it possible to do more in space with ever-smaller satellites, and Earth observation images keep getting cheaper.
- **More investors and more uses.** Private investment in space reached all-time highs of more than $70 billion in 2021 and 2022, and space-enabled applications are becoming more varied.
- **Public interest.** Space is in the news every day, and government and business leaders are considering what it could enable.

## Where will the growth come from?

The report divides the space economy into two parts:

- The **backbone**: satellites, launchers and services such as broadcast television and GPS, whose revenue goes to space hardware and service providers. It was $330 billion in 2023.
- The **reach**: revenue that other industries earn using space technology, such as navigation, weather services and parcel tracking. It was $300 billion in 2023.

The reach is projected to grow faster, at 11% a year against 7% for the backbone, and to make up nearly 60% of the space economy in 2035. Five industries are expected to generate more than 60% of the increase: supply chain and transportation; food and beverage; state-sponsored defense; retail, consumer goods and lifestyle; and digital communications.

## How certain is the projection?

It is an estimate. The report's upside estimate is $2.3 trillion by 2035, with better access to space data and lower costs of reaching space. Its lower estimate is $1.4 trillion, if access to space stalls or technology on Earth reduces the need for space-based services.

## What does the growth mean for the Space Coast?

The FSIC Master Plan describes the Space Coast as the busiest launch corridor in the world, and notes that Florida leads the world in launch cadence. Commercial, civil and defense missions are driving expansion in spacecraft manufacturing, advanced manufacturing, robotics, autonomy, and dual-use technologies.

The Master Plan also finds a severe and growing shortage of skilled workers on the Space Coast, across engineering, avionics, machining, robotics, composites, and advanced manufacturing. Launch providers, spacecraft manufacturers, and defense contractors are expanding faster than the region can produce talent.

## Is Southeast Volusia sharing in the growth?

Not yet at scale, according to the Master Plan. Southeast Volusia sits next to one of the fastest-growing aerospace and advanced manufacturing corridors in the world, but it lacks the coordinated systems, talent pipelines, and innovation infrastructure to take part fully. Without them, the Master Plan warns, the region risks becoming a commuter community to Brevard and Orlando: absorbing population growth without the high-wage jobs and employer investment that should come with it.

[The Strategic Moment](/strategic-moment/) page sets out why the Master Plan sees the next five years as the window to act.

## How is FSIC responding?

FSIC is designed to give the region what the Master Plan finds missing, through four engines:

- the **Talent Engine**, a workforce pipeline aligned to employers' needs
- the **Innovation Engine**, a civilian-facing applied innovation hub
- the **Market Engine**, which helps local companies earn certifications and enter aerospace and defense supply chains
- the **Civic Engine**, which makes the space economy visible and accessible to residents
