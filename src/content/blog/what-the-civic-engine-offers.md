## What is the Civic Engine?

The **Civic Engine** is one of FSIC’s four engines, and the one built for the public. The Master Plan calls it “the public’s front door into the space economy”: it builds community trust, regional identity and broad-based support for innovation-driven growth. Its aim, in the plan’s words, is to ensure that “innovation is visible, accessible, and meaningful to residents.”

The other three engines train workers (Talent), support applied research and prototyping (Innovation), and help local businesses enter supply chains (Market). The four are described on the [FSIC Model](/model#civic) page.

## What programs does the Master Plan describe?

- **Community STEM nights:** hands-on events for families and youth that build excitement around science, technology and space.
- **Arts + space integration:** programs that blend creativity and technology, including public art installations, space-themed exhibitions and artist-in-residence programs.
- **Public exhibitions and demonstrations:** showcases of student projects, prototypes, research outcomes and community innovations.
- **Regional storytelling and branding:** narratives that highlight local innovators, regional achievements, community impact and economic opportunity.
- **Community convenings and placemaking:** events that bring residents together to build a shared sense of identity and belonging.

STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics. The full program portfolio is on the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#portfolio) page.

## Who is it for?

Residents. The Master Plan expects residents to gain expanded access to STEM learning, workforce pathways for adults and youth, community events and exhibitions, and a shared regional narrative. FSIC’s Model page names the people the Civic Engine brings together: residents, educators, employers, artists and civic leaders.

Community organizations, nonprofits and civic groups have a role too. The plan asks them to support community engagement and outreach, collaborate on STEM events and exhibitions, provide access to underserved populations, strengthen regional storytelling and support volunteer and mentorship programs.

## What problem does it address?

The Master Plan lists five: a lack of public understanding of the space economy, a weak regional identity, limited community access to STEM experiences, insufficient civic engagement, and the need for visible public benefit.

## When will it start?

The Civic Engine’s main phase is **Phase 4: Civic Engine Expansion (Years 2–4)**. Its activities are to host community STEM nights and exhibitions, integrate arts and space programming, launch regional storytelling and branding, expand public events and convenings, and strengthen transparent governance and reporting.

Some of it starts earlier. The plan’s Year 1 milestones include hosting initial community STEM events, and the first facility is meant to be able to host early community events and STEM nights. FSIC is in Phase 1 now. Public exhibitions and arts-and-space programs are planned to launch in Year 4, with a public exhibition gallery, a community event space and STEM exploration zones for families and youth among the planned spaces. The phases are on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-4) page.

## How will FSIC show results?

The Master Plan’s measures for the Civic Engine include attendance at community STEM nights, participation in exhibitions and public events, arts-and-space program engagement, and public awareness. FSIC plans quarterly community engagement summaries and an annual civic impact report.
