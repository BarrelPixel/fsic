FSIC's Civic Engine will offer hands-on STEM learning, maker programs, community exhibitions, and arts-and-space events for residents of all ages. Programs include family STEM nights, student camps, public exhibitions that celebrate innovation and creativity, and maker spaces where residents can prototype ideas and learn new skills. The Civic Engine makes the space economy accessible and visible, inspiring students, engaging families, and showing that aerospace and advanced manufacturing are not distant industries—they are part of Southeast Volusia's future.

## What is the Civic Engine?

The Civic Engine is FSIC's community-facing program, designed to make STEM learning and the space economy accessible to everyone—not just students pursuing technical careers. While the Talent Engine trains workers and the Innovation Engine supports businesses, the Civic Engine engages the broader community through public programs, exhibitions, and events.

The Civic Engine serves three purposes:

1. **Inspire the next generation**: Show students what aerospace and advanced manufacturing careers look like, spark curiosity, and build confidence in STEM subjects
2. **Engage families**: Provide hands-on learning experiences that bring families together around science, technology, engineering, arts, and math
3. **Build regional identity**: Celebrate Southeast Volusia's connection to the space economy and position the region as a hub for innovation, not just a bedroom community

The Civic Engine is about making the invisible visible: showing residents that they belong in the space economy and that the space economy belongs in Southeast Volusia.

## What programs will the Civic Engine offer?

Civic Engine programs are designed to be accessible, hands-on, and relevant to daily life. Planned programs include:

### Family STEM nights
Monthly or quarterly events held at schools, libraries, or community centers, where families participate in hands-on activities such as:

- Building and launching model rockets
- Programming robots or drones
- Designing structures with 3D modeling software
- Exploring materials science (foam, composites, magnets)
- Learning about careers in aerospace, engineering, and advanced manufacturing

Family STEM nights are free or low-cost, require no prior knowledge, and welcome families of all backgrounds. They introduce STEM concepts in a playful, non-intimidating way.

### Student camps and workshops
Week-long summer camps or weekend workshops for middle and high school students, focused on:

- Robotics competitions (designing, building, and programming robots)
- Rocketry (building and launching model rockets, learning aerodynamics)
- Maker skills (woodworking, electronics, 3D printing, laser cutting)
- Space science (astronomy, planetary science, satellite operations)
- Arts and space (visual arts, music, storytelling inspired by space exploration)

Camps are hands-on, project-based, and led by educators, engineers, and industry mentors. Students leave with a finished project, new skills, and connections to peers who share their interests.

### Public exhibitions
Rotating exhibitions at FSIC's facilities, libraries, or partner venues, showcasing:

- Student projects from camps, schools, and competitions
- Innovations from local businesses and challenge lab projects
- Historical artifacts and stories from Florida's space program
- Arts-and-space collaborations (sculptures, installations, performances)

Exhibitions make innovation tangible and celebrate local talent. They also connect the past (Florida's space heritage) to the present (today's Space Coast growth) and the future (what residents are building).

### Maker spaces and open labs
Community access to tools and equipment for prototyping, creating, and learning:

- 3D printers, laser cutters, and CNC machines
- Electronics workbenches with soldering tools and components
- Woodworking and metalworking tools
- Sewing machines and textiles equipment
- Computers with design software (CAD, video editing, coding environments)

Maker spaces are open to residents of all ages and skill levels. Staff and volunteers provide training, safety instruction, and project support. Maker spaces foster creativity, entrepreneurship, and lifelong learning.

### Arts-and-space programs
Collaborations with artists, musicians, and cultural organizations to explore the connections between creativity and space exploration:

- Visual arts inspired by astronomy, rocket launches, or space imagery
- Music and performance celebrating Florida's space heritage
- Storytelling workshops on science fiction, space history, or future visions
- Community murals or public art installations related to innovation and exploration

Arts-and-space programs show that creativity and technical fields are not separate—they are complementary. Engineers need design thinking, and artists benefit from technical tools.

### Community science projects
Citizen science initiatives where residents contribute to real research:

- Weather monitoring and climate data collection
- Astronomy observations (tracking meteor showers, planetary transits)
- Ecology and biodiversity surveys
- Water quality testing in local waterways

Community science projects engage residents in authentic research, teach scientific methods, and contribute to publicly available datasets.

## Who will the Civic Engine serve?

The Civic Engine is designed for broad participation:

### Students (K–12)
- Students curious about STEM but not yet committed to technical careers
- Students interested in competitions (robotics, rocketry, science fairs)
- Students exploring career options and looking for hands-on experiences

### Families
- Parents and caregivers looking for educational activities to do with children
- Families seeking low-cost or free enrichment programs
- Homeschool families and learning pods

### Adults
- Career-switchers exploring new fields
- Hobbyists and makers interested in prototyping or creating
- Retirees looking for lifelong learning opportunities
- Entrepreneurs testing product ideas in maker spaces

### Educators
- Teachers seeking field trip destinations or curriculum resources
- Informal educators (librarians, youth program leaders) looking for STEM programming ideas
- Volunteers and mentors who want to share their expertise

The Civic Engine is intentionally inclusive. Programs are designed to welcome people regardless of prior knowledge, economic background, or career goals.

## How is the Civic Engine different from the Talent Engine?

The Talent Engine trains workers for specific careers. The Civic Engine inspires, engages, and educates the broader community without requiring a career commitment.

| Aspect | Talent Engine | Civic Engine |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| **Audience** | Students, job-seekers, career-switchers pursuing technical careers | Residents of all ages, interests, and backgrounds |
| **Goal** | Job-ready skills and credentials | Inspiration, exploration, lifelong learning |
| **Time commitment** | Weeks to years (micro-credentials, dual enrollment, apprenticeships) | Hours to days (events, camps, drop-in maker spaces) |
| **Cost** | May charge tuition or fees (though scholarships available) | Free or low-cost |
| **Outcome** | Employment in high-wage technical careers | Increased STEM interest, skills, and regional identity |

The Civic Engine creates the pipeline that feeds the Talent Engine: students who attend family STEM nights may later enroll in dual enrollment. Adults who prototype in maker spaces may pursue micro-credentials or start businesses. The Civic Engine builds awareness, interest, and confidence—the precursors to career commitment.

## When will the Civic Engine launch?

Civic Engine programs are part of FSIC's phased deployment. Some elements (family STEM nights, student camps) can launch early with minimal infrastructure, using schools, libraries, and partner facilities. Others (dedicated maker spaces, large-scale exhibitions) require facilities and funding, planned for later phases.

The Master Plan envisions Civic Engine programs beginning in Year 1 with pilot events and expanding as partnerships and funding grow. Early programs establish FSIC's presence in the community, build trust, and demonstrate value before larger investments are made.

## Why does the Civic Engine matter for Southeast Volusia?

The Civic Engine is not just about education—it is about identity and belonging. For decades, Southeast Volusia has been known as a quiet, beach-adjacent community. The Space Coast's boom is changing that, but many residents do not see themselves as part of the space economy.

The Civic Engine shows residents—especially young people—that they belong in this story. It makes STEM accessible, demystifies technical careers, and celebrates local innovation. It also builds social cohesion: families from different backgrounds come together around shared curiosity and creativity.

By making the space economy visible and accessible, the Civic Engine helps Southeast Volusia claim its place on the Space Coast—not as a passive neighbor, but as an active participant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will Civic Engine programs cost money?

Most Civic Engine programs will be free or low-cost. Family STEM nights, public exhibitions, and community science projects will have no admission fees. Summer camps and workshops may charge nominal fees to cover materials and staff, but scholarships will be available for families facing financial hardship. Maker spaces may charge membership or per-use fees, but FSIC will work to keep costs affordable and offer free access hours.

### Will programs be offered in Spanish or other languages?

Yes. Southeast Volusia is home to Spanish-speaking families and other language communities. FSIC will offer bilingual programming where demand exists and will recruit bilingual staff and volunteers. Materials, signage, and communications will be translated to ensure accessibility.

### Can schools bring students to Civic Engine programs as field trips?

Yes. FSIC will work with schools to offer field trip programs aligned to state science and math standards. Teachers will receive pre-visit materials, guided tours, and post-visit activities. Some programs may be offered at schools rather than requiring transportation to FSIC facilities.

### How will the Civic Engine avoid displacing existing community programs?

FSIC operates as a backbone organization, coordinating and filling gaps rather than competing. Where strong STEM programs already exist (school robotics teams, library maker programs, youth camps), FSIC will support them with funding, mentorship, or shared equipment. FSIC will launch new programs only where gaps exist or demand exceeds current capacity.
