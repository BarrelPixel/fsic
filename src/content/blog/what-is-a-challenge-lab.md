## What is a challenge lab?

A **challenge lab** is a program in which a team works on a real problem brought by someone who needs it solved. In FSIC’s Master Plan, “teams work on real aerospace and advanced manufacturing problems provided by employers, universities, or federal partners. Outcomes include prototypes, research findings, and new technologies.”

The plan describes the purpose as “structured, team-based problem-solving” that turns “real industry challenges into prototypes, applied research, and emerging technologies.”

## Where do challenge labs fit in FSIC?

Challenge labs are the first core function of the **Innovation Engine**, one of FSIC’s four engines. The Master Plan calls the Innovation Engine “the region’s first civilian-facing applied innovation hub,” where students, educators, researchers, startups and industry partners work together on aerospace and advanced technology challenges.

The Innovation Engine’s other planned programs are:

- A rapid prototyping studio, with 3D printers, CNC machines, electronics benches and materials testing tools
- Innovation sprints and hackathons
- Applied research partnerships with universities and federal agencies

The full list is on the [FSIC Model](/model#innovation) page and the [Programs & Facilities](/programs-facilities/#portfolio) page.

## Who takes part?

The Master Plan gives each partner a role:

- **Employers and industry** provide challenge lab problems and innovation projects.
- **State colleges and universities** provide faculty expertise for challenge labs.
- **K–12 school districts** provide student access to challenge labs and innovation programs.
- **Federal and state agencies** participate in challenge labs and innovation programs.

No partners have been announced for FSIC’s challenge labs.

## What problem are they meant to solve?

The Master Plan finds that the Space Coast has no civilian innovation hub, limited applied research capacity, no shared prototyping or testing facilities, and weak collaboration between industry and universities. Challenge labs, with the rest of the Innovation Engine, are FSIC’s planned response.

## When will FSIC’s challenge labs start?

Challenge labs are part of **Phase 2: Innovation Engine Activation (Years 1–2)**. FSIC is in Phase 1, which launches the Talent Engine. The Master Plan’s activities for Phase 2 begin with “launch challenge labs and innovation sprints,” and its first expected outcome is “first prototypes developed through challenge labs.” The plan’s year-by-year roadmap places the launch in Year 2.

The planned space for that phase includes challenge labs for student and industry collaboration, a rapid prototyping studio and small research and testing labs. Each phase is set out on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-2) page.

## How will FSIC report on them?

The Master Plan’s measures for the Innovation Engine include the number of challenge labs conducted and the prototypes developed and tested, reported in semiannual innovation reports and an annual research and development summary.
