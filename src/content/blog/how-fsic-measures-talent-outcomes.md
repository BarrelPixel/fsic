FSIC will measure Talent Engine outcomes through metrics that matter to participants, employers, and funders: program completions, job placement rates within six months, starting wages and wage growth over time, employer satisfaction with graduates, and demographic equity. These outcomes will be tracked using state wage records, employer surveys, and credential data, and reported annually in public reports that show both progress and areas for improvement.

## What outcomes will FSIC track for the Talent Engine?

The Talent Engine's success is measured by whether residents gain skills, get hired, earn higher wages, and build careers. FSIC will track five core outcomes:

### 1. Program completions
- **What it measures**: Number of participants who complete micro-credentials, dual enrollment pathways, apprenticeships, or other training programs
- **Why it matters**: Completion is the first step toward employment. High completion rates indicate that programs are accessible, relevant, and well-designed.
- **Data source**: Training providers (community colleges, workforce boards) report completions to FSIC and state databases.

### 2. Job placement rates
- **What it measures**: Percentage of program completers employed in their field within six months of completion
- **Why it matters**: Training that does not lead to employment wastes participants' time and funders' money. Placement rates show whether programs align with employer demand.
- **Data source**: Florida's Department of Economic Opportunity (DEO) Unemployment Insurance (UI) wage records, matched to program participants with their consent.

### 3. Starting wages
- **What it measures**: Median starting wage for program graduates, by occupation and program
- **Why it matters**: Training should lead to jobs that pay enough to support a family. Starting wages show whether programs deliver on the promise of economic mobility.
- **Data source**: DEO wage records, reported quarterly for employed participants.

### 4. Wage growth over time
- **What it measures**: Change in wages for participants one year and three years after program completion, compared to their pre-training wages
- **Why it matters**: Long-term wage growth shows whether training creates sustainable career pathways, not just one-time wage bumps.
- **Data source**: DEO wage records, tracked longitudinally for consenting participants.

### 5. Employer satisfaction
- **What it measures**: Employer ratings of graduates' preparedness, technical skills, work ethic, and retention
- **Why it matters**: Employers hire graduates and shape program content. Their feedback ensures training meets real-world needs.
- **Data source**: Employer surveys conducted six months and one year post-hire.

In addition to these core metrics, FSIC will track:

- **Demographic equity**: Participation and outcomes by race, gender, age, and income level, ensuring programs serve underrepresented groups
- **Credential attainment**: Type and number of industry-recognized credentials earned (e.g., FAA certifications, AWS welding certifications, college degrees)
- **Retention and advancement**: Percentage of graduates still employed after one year, and percentage promoted or receiving raises

## How will outcomes be verified?

FSIC will use third-party data sources to verify outcomes independently, not relying solely on self-reported data:

### State wage records
Florida's DEO maintains Unemployment Insurance (UI) wage records for nearly all employed residents. FSIC will partner with DEO to access these records for program participants who consent. This verifies:
- Whether a participant is employed
- Their employer and industry
- Their quarterly wages

Wage records are the gold standard for workforce program evaluation because they are objective, comprehensive, and not subject to participant recall or bias.

### Credential data
Community colleges, workforce boards, and certification bodies report completions and credentials to state and federal databases. FSIC will track participants' credentials through these systems.

### Employer surveys
FSIC will survey employers who hire program graduates six months and one year post-hire, asking:
- Is the graduate still employed?
- How well did the training prepare them for the job?
- What additional skills or knowledge would improve future graduates' readiness?

Employer feedback provides qualitative context that wage records alone cannot capture.

## What are performance benchmarks?

FSIC will set performance targets for each metric based on industry standards and comparable programs:

| Metric | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------|
| Completion rate | ≥ 70% of enrolled participants complete the program |
| Placement rate | ≥ 75% of completers employed in their field within six months |
| Starting wage | Median starting wage ≥ $18/hour for entry-level technical roles; ≥ $25/hour for advanced roles |
| Wage growth (one year) | ≥ 20% increase over pre-training wages |
| Employer satisfaction | ≥ 85% of employers rate graduates as "prepared" or "very prepared" |
| Demographic equity | Participation rates for underrepresented groups match or exceed their share of the regional population |

These benchmarks reflect expectations from federal workforce grants, state performance accountability systems, and best practices in the field. FSIC will report actual performance against these targets annually.

## How will outcomes be reported?

FSIC will publish an annual Talent Engine report that includes:

### Quantitative outcomes (by program)
- Enrollments, completions, and completion rates
- Job placements and placement rates
- Median starting wages and wage ranges
- Wage growth comparisons (pre- vs. post-training)
- Demographics of participants and outcomes by subgroup

### Qualitative outcomes
- Participant success stories: backgrounds, training paths, career outcomes
- Employer testimonials and feedback
- Lessons learned and program improvements

### Comparison to benchmarks
- Actual performance vs. targets for each metric
- Trends over time (improving, stable, or declining)
- Peer comparisons (how FSIC's outcomes compare to similar programs in Florida or nationally)

The report will be published on FSIC's website, shared with funders and partners, and presented to the Board of Directors and Multi-Institution Advisory Council. Outcome data will also be included in FSIC's overall annual impact report.

## How will FSIC use outcome data to improve programs?

Outcome measurement is not just for accountability—it drives continuous improvement:

### If completion rates are low:
- Survey participants who drop out to understand barriers (transportation, childcare, cost, program fit)
- Adjust program length, schedule, or delivery format (online, hybrid, evening classes)
- Strengthen participant support (tutoring, career counseling, emergency financial assistance)

### If placement rates are low:
- Survey employers to identify skill gaps or hiring barriers
- Strengthen job placement services (resume help, interview coaching, employer connections)
- Revise curriculum to better align with employer needs

### If starting wages are below target:
- Work with employers to validate that wages match the skills trained
- Adjust program focus to higher-wage occupations or specializations
- Ensure participants understand the career ladder and how to negotiate wages

### If employer satisfaction is low:
- Solicit detailed feedback on what graduates lack
- Adjust curriculum, add hands-on training, or extend program length
- Strengthen employer partnerships to ensure real-time input on program design

### If equity gaps exist:
- Increase outreach to underrepresented groups
- Remove barriers (application fees, GED requirements, drug testing where not job-related)
- Provide wraparound services (transportation stipends, childcare subsidies, career navigation)

Data-driven improvement ensures the Talent Engine stays relevant, effective, and responsive to participants' and employers' needs.

## How does FSIC balance short-term and long-term outcomes?

Some outcomes are visible within months (completions, placements), while others take years (wage growth, career advancement). FSIC will report both:

### Short-term indicators (Year 1–2):
- Enrollments and completions
- Initial job placements
- Employer satisfaction at hire

These show that programs are launching and producing graduates.

### Medium-term indicators (Year 2–3):
- One-year wage growth
- Retention rates (still employed after one year)
- Credential stackability (graduates earning additional credentials)

These show that training creates sustainable career pathways.

### Long-term indicators (Year 3–5+):
- Three-year wage growth
- Career advancement (promotions, raises, leadership roles)
- Alumni outcomes (where graduates are five years post-training)

These show whether FSIC is building a skilled, stable workforce that lifts families and strengthens the regional economy.

FSIC will report progress on all timelines, giving stakeholders a realistic picture of what is working and what will take time to materialize.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will participants' personal data be shared publicly?

No. Outcome reports will present aggregate data (medians, percentages, totals) and anonymized case studies. Individual participants' names, wages, and employers will not be disclosed without their consent. Data sharing with DEO and other agencies complies with federal and state privacy laws (FERPA, WIOA confidentiality rules).

### How does FSIC compare to other workforce programs?

FSIC's outcome metrics align with standards used by community colleges, workforce boards, and state accountability systems. Completion rates, placement rates, and wage gains are common metrics across the field. FSIC's use of state wage records for verification is considered best practice. Comparisons to peer programs (in Florida and nationally) will be included in annual reports where data is available.

### What if a participant gets a job unrelated to their training?

This counts as a non-placement. FSIC tracks whether participants are employed in their field of training (occupation codes matched to program type). A CNC machinist trainee hired as a retail cashier is employed but not placed. This distinction shows whether training aligns with jobs available and whether participants value the credential enough to seek work in that field.

### Can outcomes be tracked for participants who leave the region?

State wage records only capture employment within Florida. Participants who move out of state or become self-employed are lost to follow-up unless they self-report outcomes. FSIC will note this limitation in reports and may supplement wage-record data with participant surveys to capture out-of-state or self-employment outcomes.
