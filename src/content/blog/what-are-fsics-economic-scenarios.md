## What are the three scenarios?

The FSIC Master Plan evaluates FSIC's projected 25-year impact under three scenarios: **Baseline (No FSIC)**, **Basic FSIC (FSIC 1.0)** and **Extended FSIC (FSIC 2.0)**. Comparing them shows how the region's economic outlook changes depending on whether FSIC is pursued, and at what scale.

- **Baseline (No FSIC)** is the comparison case. The Master Plan describes it as "a slow growth future with limited job creation, modest fiscal gains, and a decades long buildout."
- **FSIC 1.0** is the foundational phase. It activates the region's economic engine through four coordinated program areas: Talent, Innovation, Market, and Civic Advancement. The Master Plan calls it "a low-risk, high-leverage platform that organizes the region, attracts early partners, and prepares the groundwork for larger capital investments in FSIC 2.0."
- **FSIC 2.0** is the expanded model: a fully realized innovation district with anchor institutions, high tech employers, and a convention and performing arts center. In the Master Plan's analysis it generates the strongest economic output, the highest wage job creation, and the largest inflow of external funding.

The four program areas are the engines described on [The FSIC Model](/model/) page.

## How do the scenarios differ?

They differ in scale. In the Master Plan's comparison, each FSIC scenario projects more economic output, external funding, fiscal impact, wages and annual visitors than the Baseline, and FSIC 2.0 projects the most on every measure. The [Economic Impact](/economic-impact/#scenarios) page shows the full comparison, scenario by scenario, as the Master Plan's chart gives it.

The Master Plan presents FSIC 1.0 as groundwork for FSIC 2.0: the program areas come first, and the larger capital investments of an innovation district follow.

## Which scenario do the headline figures describe?

The figures FSIC cites describe **FSIC 2.0**, the Extended FSIC scenario, over 25 years:

- **$10.2–$12.4 billion** in economic output
- **74,500–79,500** job-years supported
- **$1.35–$1.55 billion** in new wages
- **$690–$910 million** in fiscal revenue
- **$800 million–$1.56 billion** in external funding potential

These are estimates based upon assumptions that may or may not be accurate over time. A job-year is one job held for one year; [What Is a Job-Year?](/blog/what-is-a-job-year/) explains how to read that figure.

## Why are the figures ranges?

The Master Plan gives every figure as a low and a high estimate, and FSIC always shows both ends. Quoting only the upper end would overstate what the analysis says. The Master Plan does not set out the model or the assumptions behind each range.

## What does this mean for FSIC's plans?

The scenarios describe possible outcomes, not commitments. FSIC's five-year plan starts with the Talent Engine and adds the other engines in phases, as the [Deployment](/deployment/) page describes. Whether the region reaches the outcomes of either FSIC scenario depends on how that plan, and the investment behind it, develops.
