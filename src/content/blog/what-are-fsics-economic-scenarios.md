FSIC's Master Plan analyzes three scenarios: FSIC 0.5 (modest implementation with limited programs), FSIC 1.0 (core engines fully built), and FSIC 2.0 (full implementation including the campus and all programs). The scenarios differ in scope, timeline, and outcomes. The widely cited job and economic figures—74,500–79,500 job-years and $10.2–$12.4 billion in economic impact over 25 years—come from FSIC 2.0, the full-implementation scenario, and represent estimates, not guarantees.

## What are the three scenarios?

The Master Plan presents three implementation scenarios to show how FSIC's economic impact scales with the level of investment and programmatic scope:

- **FSIC 0.5 (Pilot Implementation)**: A limited version of FSIC with small-scale pilot programs in workforce training and business support. No campus, no major facilities, and no Innovation Engine. This scenario tests the model with minimal risk but generates modest outcomes.

- **FSIC 1.0 (Core Implementation)**: The four engines—Talent, Innovation, Market, and Civic—are fully built and operational, but without a dedicated campus. Programs operate through partnerships with existing institutions and shared facilities. This scenario delivers substantial regional impact at moderate cost.

- **FSIC 2.0 (Extended Implementation)**: Full implementation, including a physical campus, expanded programs across all four engines, and long-term capacity for research, commercialization, and large-scale workforce development. This is the scenario that delivers the maximum projected economic impact over 25 years.

The scenarios are not mutually exclusive or sequential. FSIC could operate at the 1.0 level indefinitely, or move from 1.0 to 2.0 as funding and partnerships develop. The purpose of modeling three scenarios is to help policymakers, funders, and the community understand the trade-offs between investment and outcomes.

## How do the scenarios differ?

The three scenarios differ in scope, timeline, funding requirements, and projected outcomes:

| Aspect | FSIC 0.5 (Pilot) | FSIC 1.0 (Core) | FSIC 2.0 (Extended) |
|--------|------------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Workforce programs** | Small pilot cohorts | Full-scale training across all four engines | Campus-based programs, apprenticeships, and teacher training |
| **Innovation programs** | No Innovation Engine | Challenge labs and prototyping partnerships | Dedicated research facilities and commercialization support |
| **Facilities** | None; virtual organization | Leased or shared spaces | Physical campus with labs, classrooms, and community spaces |
| **Funding requirement** | Modest (grants and local support) | Moderate (public-private blend) | Substantial (state, federal, philanthropic, and private investment) |
| **Timeline to full operation** | 1–2 years | 3–5 years | 5–10 years |
| **Economic impact (25 years)** | Modest, not modeled in detail | Moderate | $10.2–$12.4 billion in total economic output |
| **Job-years supported (25 years)** | Small number, not modeled in detail | Moderate | 74,500–79,500 job-years |

The Master Plan emphasizes that even FSIC 0.5 delivers measurable value to the region. The 1.0 and 2.0 scenarios build on that foundation, increasing reach and impact as funding and partnerships grow.

## Which scenario do the headline figures describe?

The widely cited figures—**$10.2–$12.4 billion in economic output** and **74,500–79,500 job-years over 25 years**—describe FSIC 2.0, the full-implementation scenario.

This is the scenario in which:

- FSIC operates a physical campus with research labs, prototyping facilities, and classrooms.
- All four engines—Talent, Innovation, Market, and Civic—are fully built and sustained over decades.
- FSIC trains thousands of workers annually, supports hundreds of businesses, and anchors a regional innovation ecosystem.

The 2.0 scenario requires substantial investment from state and federal sources, philanthropic foundations, and private partners. It is the vision the Master Plan describes, but it is not guaranteed. Whether FSIC reaches 2.0 depends on the pace of partnerships, funding, and community support.

The Master Plan is transparent about this: "These projections represent the potential economic impact of FSIC if fully implemented and sustained over 25 years. Actual outcomes will depend on the pace of implementation, the level of investment, and external economic conditions."

## Why does the Master Plan show ranges instead of single numbers?

The economic figures are shown as ranges—$10.2–$12.4 billion, not $12.4 billion alone—because they are estimates based on assumptions about future conditions. Ranges communicate uncertainty and provide a more honest picture than a single number.

The ranges reflect:

- **Different assumptions about participation rates**: How many students will enroll? How many businesses will engage with FSIC's programs? These numbers depend on factors FSIC cannot control, such as population growth, employer demand, and competing opportunities.
- **Economic modeling conventions**: Economic impact studies typically model low, medium, and high scenarios. The ranges in the Master Plan represent the spread between conservative and optimistic assumptions.
- **External variables**: The Space Coast's economic growth, federal funding availability, and workforce migration all affect the outcomes. The ranges acknowledge that FSIC operates in a dynamic environment.

Showing ranges is standard practice in economic impact studies. It is more credible than claiming precision where none exists, and it allows readers to make their own judgments about which end of the range is more likely.

## What would each scenario require?

Each scenario has different funding, partnership, and timeline requirements:

### FSIC 0.5 (Pilot):
- **Funding**: Primarily grants, local government support, and small philanthropic contributions (modest initial funding).
- **Partnerships**: One or two community colleges, a school district, and a handful of employers.
- **Timeline**: Launch pilot programs in Year 1; evaluate results in Year 2.
- **Risk**: Low. If the pilots fail, the loss is modest. If they succeed, they provide proof of concept for scaling up.

### FSIC 1.0 (Core):
- **Funding**: Blended public-private investment, including state workforce grants, federal programs (such as NSF or EDA), and significant philanthropic and employer contributions (tens of millions of dollars over five years).
- **Partnerships**: Multi-institution consortia including community colleges, universities, school districts, workforce boards, and dozens of employers.
- **Timeline**: Full operation by Year 5.
- **Risk**: Moderate. Requires sustained funding and coordination, but does not depend on building a campus.

### FSIC 2.0 (Extended):
- **Funding**: Substantial investment from state capital appropriations, federal research and infrastructure programs, large philanthropic foundations, and private partners (over one hundred million dollars over ten years).
- **Partnerships**: Anchored by major universities, Space Florida, the Department of Defense, NASA, and regional economic development agencies.
- **Facilities**: A campus with labs, maker spaces, classrooms, and public-facing innovation centers.
- **Timeline**: Full operation by Year 10.
- **Risk**: High. Requires long-term commitment, major funding, and alignment of many stakeholders. The payoff is proportionally higher.

The phased approach allows FSIC to start small, demonstrate results, and build credibility before pursuing the larger vision.

## Are the economic projections guaranteed?

No. The Master Plan is explicit: "These projections are estimates based on modeling assumptions and comparable case studies. They are not guarantees."

Economic impact projections are forecasts, not promises. They are based on:

- **Input-output models** that estimate how spending on workforce training, research, and business support ripples through the regional economy.
- **Case studies** of similar organizations, such as regional innovation hubs, workforce development centers, and university-anchored ecosystems.
- **Assumptions** about participation rates, funding levels, and the regional economy's capacity to absorb new workers and businesses.

Actual outcomes depend on factors FSIC cannot control, including macroeconomic conditions, federal policy, the pace of the space industry's growth, and the region's ability to attract and retain talent. The projections represent FSIC's potential impact if it is well-funded, well-executed, and sustained over decades—not a prediction of what will happen regardless of effort or context.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which scenario is FSIC pursuing?

FSIC is starting with pilot programs (similar to the 0.5 scenario) and working toward the 1.0 scenario as partnerships and funding develop. The 2.0 scenario is the long-term vision, but reaching it depends on demonstrating success at smaller scales first. The Master Plan describes this as "building momentum through results," not committing to a timeline before the foundation is in place.

### Why model a 0.5 scenario if FSIC aims for 1.0 or 2.0?

Modeling the 0.5 scenario shows what FSIC can deliver with modest investment, which is valuable for early funders and partners who want proof of concept before committing larger resources. It also provides a fallback: if FSIC cannot secure funding for the 1.0 or 2.0 scenarios, the 0.5 version still delivers value to the region. Transparency about the range of possibilities is more credible than claiming one outcome is certain.

### How were the economic figures calculated?

The figures were calculated using IMPLAN, an economic modeling software widely used for impact studies. The model estimates direct, indirect, and induced economic effects based on spending on workforce programs, research and development, facility operations, and business support. The assumptions and methodology are detailed in the Master Plan's economic analysis section (pages 7–8). FSIC can provide the full methodology to policymakers and funders upon request.

### Can FSIC stay at the 1.0 level, or does it have to reach 2.0?

FSIC can operate indefinitely at the 1.0 level. The 1.0 scenario delivers substantial regional impact without requiring a campus or the long-term funding commitments that 2.0 demands. The decision to pursue 2.0 depends on whether the region, funders, and partners believe the additional investment is worth the additional outcomes. The phased approach gives FSIC and the community time to make that decision based on results, not speculation.
