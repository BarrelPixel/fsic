## What is the Florida Space Innovation Center?

The **Florida Space Innovation Center (FSIC)** is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization based in New Smyrna Beach, Florida. It is a regional workforce and innovation initiative for Southeast Volusia.

FSIC's [FAQ](/faq/#what-is-fsic) describes it as "a regional innovation and workforce engine designed to connect residents, students, educators, small businesses, and employers to the rapidly expanding space and advanced technology economy." The same answer says what kind of thing FSIC is: "It is not a single building, but a coordinated platform that brings together universities, industry, government, and community partners to accelerate economic activity and expand opportunity."

The FAQ also states the purpose behind it. FSIC is rooted in "the city's goal to diversify its economy and bring higher wage jobs to local residents."

## What does FSIC do?

FSIC works through four engines. An engine is one area of FSIC's work, with its own programs and partners. The FAQ lists them:

- **Talent:** "developing a cradle-to-career STEM and technical workforce". STEM stands for science, technology, engineering and mathematics.
- **Innovation:** "challenge labs, prototyping, and applied research".
- **Market:** "helping small businesses and startups enter aerospace and advanced manufacturing supply chains".
- **Civic:** "community STEM engagement, arts integration, and public programming".

The engines start in order, over five phases. The Talent Engine is first, in Phase 1 (Year 1). The Innovation Engine follows in Years 1–2, the Market Engine in Years 2–3 and the Civic Engine's expansion in Years 2–4. Phase 5 (Years 3–5) brings the four together. Each engine is described on [The FSIC Model](/model/) page, and the reason for the order in [Why Does FSIC Start with the Talent Engine?](/blog/why-fsic-starts-with-talent/)

FSIC does this work as a coordinator. Its Master Plan calls FSIC "the region's neutral backbone organization", coordinating K–12 districts, state colleges, universities, workforce boards, employers, civic partners and community organizations (p. 3). A backbone organization is a separate organization that coordinates the work of many partners toward a shared goal; see [What Is a Backbone Organization?](/blog/what-is-a-backbone-organization/)

## Where does FSIC stand today?

As of November 2026, FSIC is in Phase 1 of its Master Plan: Foundation & Talent Engine Launch, which covers Year 1. Everything after Phase 1 is planned, not under way.

- **Launched:** FSIC launched on May 25, 2026, in New Smyrna Beach.
- **Tax status:** FSIC's legal name is Florida Space Innovation Center Inc. Its IRS determination letter, dated October 20, 2026, recognizes it as exempt under section 501(c)(3), effective July 1, 2026, and classifies it as a public charity.
- **Location:** FSIC's FAQ states that it "is a virtual organization located in New Smyrna Beach." A future site in Volusia County "will be determined through a collaborative process with community members, civic leaders, industry partners, and other stakeholders" ([FAQ](/faq/#location)).
- **Programs:** Phase 1's activities in the Master Plan are to launch micro-credential academies, establish dual-enrollment STEM pathways, begin teacher upskilling programs, deploy the Regional Talent Marketplace and form employer advisory councils (p. 13). FSIC has not announced opening dates or locations for them.
- **Organization:** Phase 1 also includes securing initial philanthropic and public funding and establishing FSIC's governance, bylaws and board independence (p. 13).

The phases are set out on the [Phased Deployment](/deployment/#phase-1) page.

## Who leads FSIC?

FSIC was founded by John Joaquin, who chaired the City of New Smyrna Beach's Economic Development Task Force and the Southeast Volusia Chamber of Commerce's Advocacy & Economic Development committee, "where the idea of the FSIC was developed" ([FAQ](/faq/#leadership)).

As of November 2026, FSIC has announced these members of its Board of Directors: Erik Ahlgren (Board Chair), Beth Hamilton, Jessie Thompson (a member of the Volusia County School Board), Diezel DePew (Mayor of Edgewater) and Ryan Trine (CEO of R3Doubt). Jake Johansson has joined FSIC's Founders Council. The board is listed on the [Governance & Transparency](/governance/#structure) page, and each appointment is announced on the [News](/news/) page.

## Where did FSIC come from?

FSIC's FAQ traces its origins to "the City of New Smyrna Beach's 2023 Economic Development Task Force formed to develop recommendations for economic diversification and better career pathways for local residents."

FSIC launched on May 25, 2026. Its launch release describes FSIC as beginning in New Smyrna Beach and "intentionally designed to scale across the region." The release states FSIC's mission: "to ensure that local values, local voices, and local priorities shape the opportunities emerging around the Space Coast."

## What is FSIC not?

- **Not a single building.** FSIC describes itself as "a coordinated platform", and today it is a virtual organization.
- **Not a government agency.** FSIC is a nonprofit corporation that the IRS recognizes as a 501(c)(3) public charity. Its origins are in a city task force, and its Master Plan lists cities and counties among its partners (p. 23). What the status means is explained in [What Does FSIC's 501(c)(3) Status Mean?](/blog/what-fsic-501c3-status-means/)
- **Not a replacement for schools or colleges.** FSIC's FAQ states: "It does not replace existing institutions — it strengthens them." It describes FSIC's role as "to coordinate, align, and accelerate — not duplicate."
- **Not a developer.** FSIC's FAQ states that "FSIC does not endorse specific projects or drive development", and that it "does not influence or promote" the region's approved development decisions. See [What Is FSIC's Relationship With Developers?](/blog/fsic-relationship-with-developers/)
