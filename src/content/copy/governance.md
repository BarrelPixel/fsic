## 01 · A NEUTRAL BACKBONE ORGANIZATION

**Headline:** A Neutral Backbone Organization

**Thesis (label column):** FSIC's role is to coordinate, align, and accelerate — not duplicate.

**Body:**
FSIC is a neutral backbone organization that coordinates the work of schools, colleges, universities, employers, and civic partners. It does not replace existing institutions — it strengthens them.

FSIC fills gaps, aligns stakeholders, and builds the connective infrastructure that no single organization can create alone.

FSIC is intentionally designed to amplify, not compete with, existing institutions. It:
- Co-brands programs with partners
- Shares credit, curriculum, and facilities
- Strengthens enrollment in partner institutions
- Builds pathways that benefit all stakeholders

> **Source:** fsic.space FAQ, two answers combined (verbatim). The closing line of the second answer is the thesis.

## 02 · GOVERNANCE AND ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE

**Headline:** Governance and Organizational Structure

**Thesis (label column):** The governance model ensures that no single individual, partner, or sector can dominate decision-making.

> **Source:** Master Plan p. 15 (verbatim)

**Lead:** FSIC's leadership model is intentionally broad. A cross-sector Board of Directors and a Multi-Institution Advisory Council — representing education, industry, workforce, civic, and government partners — will be featured on the FSIC website as they are formally seated. This structure ensures FSIC remains a neutral, community-anchored institution.

> **Source:** fsic.space FAQ, "Who leads FSIC?" (verbatim)

### Board of Directors

The Board of Directors serves as the governing body responsible for strategic oversight, fiduciary stewardship, and organizational accountability. The board is intentionally structured to ensure independence, diversity of expertise, and representation across key regional sectors.

**Composition:**
- Independent community leaders
- Industry and employer representatives
- Education and workforce leaders
- Economic development partners
- Philanthropic and civic leaders
- Non-voting ex officio roles for key partners

**Board independence:**
- Independent board chair
- Term limits
- Conflict-of-interest policies
- Balanced representation across sectors
- Clear separation between governance and operations

**Responsibilities:**
- Strategic direction and mission alignment
- Fiscal oversight and budget approval
- CEO hiring, evaluation, and succession planning
- Policy development and compliance
- Oversight of major partnerships and agreements
- Ensuring transparency and public accountability

> **Source:** Master Plan pp. 15–16 (verbatim)

### Executive Leadership

The executive team is responsible for day-to-day operations, program execution, partnership management, and organizational growth. The CEO leads the organization with support from directors overseeing each engine.

**Key roles:**
- Chief Executive Officer
- Director of Talent Engine
- Director of Innovation Engine
- Director of Market Engine
- Director of Civic Engine
- Director of Operations and Finance

**Responsibilities:**
- Implementing board-approved strategy
- Managing staff and operations
- Building partnerships with employers, schools, and universities
- Securing funding and managing grants
- Ensuring program quality and impact
- Reporting outcomes to the board and public

> **Source:** Master Plan p. 16 (verbatim)

### Advisory Councils

Advisory councils provide specialized expertise, employer input, and community insight. They ensure that FSIC remains aligned with industry needs, educational priorities, and community expectations.

**Councils:**
- Employer Advisory Council
- Education and Workforce Advisory Council
- Innovation and Research Advisory Council
- Community and Civic Advisory Council
- Small Business and Supplier Advisory Council

**Functions:**
- Provide technical expertise
- Validate curriculum and program design
- Identify emerging industry needs
- Support employer recruitment and partnerships
- Strengthen community engagement

> **Source:** Master Plan p. 16 (verbatim)

### Board of Advisors

The Board of Advisors provides high-level guidance, strategic insight, and relationship-driven support to the CEO and leadership team.

**Composition:**
- Influential community and regional leaders
- Senior executives from key industries
- Philanthropic and civic influencers
- University, research, and innovation partners
- Entrepreneurs, founders, and small-business leaders
- Former elected officials and policy influencers
- Strategic connectors who open networks and opportunities
- Subject-matter experts aligned to FSIC's four engines

**Responsibilities:**
- Open doors to employers, funders, and institutional partners
- Support resource development and philanthropic outreach
- Offer insight on regional trends, opportunities, and risks
- Serve as ambassadors for FSIC in the community and across sectors
- Strengthen FSIC's visibility, credibility, and influence
- Advise on program relevance and emerging industry needs
- Support special initiatives, events, and public engagement

> **Source:** Master Plan pp. 16–17 (verbatim)
> **⚠ Review:** "Influencers" appears twice. Consider "leaders" to keep the institutional tone (`docs/03-content-guidelines.md`).

## 03 · PUBLIC REPORTING AND TRANSPARENCY

**Headline:** Public Reporting and Transparency

**Thesis (label column):** Transparent reporting strengthens credibility, supports fundraising, and ensures FSIC remains accountable to the region it serves.

**Lead:** The Florida Space Innovation Center is committed to rigorous evaluation, transparent reporting, and continuous improvement. The evaluation framework measures outcomes across workforce development, innovation, commercialization, and community engagement. This approach ensures accountability to funders, partners, and the public while enabling data-driven decision-making and long-term impact.

**Public reporting:**
- Annual Impact Report
- Public dashboards on FSIC's website
- Community presentations and exhibitions
- Grant-specific reporting for funders
- Regular updates to local governments and partners

**Compliance:**
- Annual audits
- Conflict-of-interest disclosures
- Public reporting and transparency
- Grant compliance and reporting
- Ethical fundraising policies

**Evaluation principles:**
- Measure what matters most to employers, educators, funders, and the community
- Use data to drive continuous improvement
- Report outcomes transparently and consistently
- Align metrics with state and federal funding requirements
- Demonstrate clear return on investment

> **Source:** Master Plan pp. 17 (compliance framework) and 31 (verbatim)

## 05 · THE ROLE OF DEVELOPERS

**Headline:** What Is the Role of Developers?

**Thesis (label column):** Developers play a collaborative — not controlling — role in FSIC's work.

**Body:**
FSIC is an independent, community-aligned organization, and we recognize that many residents value slow growth or prefer no additional growth at all. Large developments in the region have already been approved, and FSIC does not influence or promote those decisions.

Our role is to help ensure that what happens within that approved growth reflects community priorities by steering opportunities toward high-wage, future-focused industries rather than low-value or high-impact uses. Developers may help create spaces that support training, innovation, and community engagement, but FSIC does not endorse specific projects or drive development.

Our focus is protecting community character while shaping the future economy in ways that benefit the people who already live here.

> **Source:** fsic.space FAQ, "What is the role of developers in the FSIC?" (verbatim). An important trust statement; consistent with the decision not to name a site (audit §3.3).
> **⚠ Review:** The FAQ speaks as "we"; the rest of the page is in the third person. Fine as FSIC's own statement, or change to "FSIC" throughout.
