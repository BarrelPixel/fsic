## THE FIVE PHASES

For each phase: name, timeframe, overview, core activities, key outcomes, and the facilities focus for that phase.

> **Source:** Master Plan pp. 13–14, "Phase Overview / Core Activities / Key Outcomes" table (verbatim). Facilities lines: Master Plan p. 18 (verbatim). Phase 1's timeframe ("Year 1") is from p. 18; the p. 13 table cell is cut off in the PDF.

### Phase 1: Foundation & Talent Engine Launch — Year 1

**Overview:** Establishes FSIC's operational foundation and launches the Talent Engine, the first and most critical component of the model. This phase focuses on building credibility, demonstrating early wins, and establishing the partnerships required for long-term success.

**Core activities:**
- Launch micro-credential academies
- Establish dual-enrollment STEM pathways
- Begin teacher upskilling programs
- Deploy the Regional Talent Marketplace
- Form employer advisory councils
- Secure initial philanthropic and public funding
- Establish governance, bylaws, and board independence

**Key outcomes:**
- First cohort of students enrolled in micro-credentials
- Employer-validated curriculum in place
- Teacher professional development underway
- Regional Talent Marketplace operational
- Strong early partnerships with K–12, colleges, and employers
- FSIC recognized as the region's workforce backbone

**Facilities:** Focuses on establishing a functional, cost-efficient startup facility that enables immediate program delivery.

### Phase 2: Innovation Engine Activation — Years 1–2

**Overview:** Introduces the Innovation Engine, creating the region's first civilian-facing applied innovation hub. This phase expands FSIC's impact beyond workforce development into applied research, prototyping, and dual-use technology development.

**Core activities:**
- Launch challenge labs and innovation sprints
- Establish rapid prototyping studio
- Form university and federal research partnerships
- Host community innovation events
- Begin dual-use technology development programs
- Expand employer engagement

**Key outcomes:**
- First prototypes developed through challenge labs
- University partnerships formalized
- Applied research projects underway
- Increased visibility and community engagement
- Stronger employer recruitment and retention

**Facilities:** Enables FSIC to support applied innovation, dual-use technology development, and deeper employer engagement.

### Phase 3: Market Engine Deployment — Years 2–3

**Overview:** Activates the Market Engine, supporting local businesses, startups, and entrepreneurs through commercialization, supplier readiness, and federal funding pathways.

**Core activities:**
- Launch supplier-readiness bootcamps
- Provide certification support (AS9100, ITAR, CMMC)
- Establish SBIR/STTR navigation services
- Create startup acceleration programs
- Build contract matchmaking and procurement pathways

**Key outcomes:**
- Local companies entering aerospace and defense supply chains
- Increased SBIR/STTR submissions and awards
- Startups accelerated and supported
- Stronger local supplier base
- Increased economic diversification and resilience

**Facilities:** Introduces facilities that support commercialization, supplier readiness, and startup acceleration.

### Phase 4: Civic Engine Expansion — Years 2–4

**Overview:** Expands the Civic Engine, strengthening community engagement, regional identity, and public trust. This phase ensures that innovation is visible, accessible, and meaningful to residents.

**Core activities:**
- Host community STEM nights and exhibitions
- Integrate arts and space programming
- Launch regional storytelling and branding initiatives
- Expand public events and convenings
- Strengthen transparent governance and reporting

**Key outcomes:**
- Increased community participation
- Stronger regional identity
- Expanded access to STEM experiences
- Greater civic trust and transparency
- Broad public support for innovation-driven growth

**Facilities:** Expands FSIC's public-facing spaces to support community engagement, exhibitions, and regional identity building.

### Phase 5: Full Ecosystem Integration — Years 3–5

**Overview:** Integrates all four engines into a cohesive regional innovation ecosystem capable of competing for major federal and state investments. This phase positions FSIC as a durable regional institution.

**Core activities:**
- Integrate Talent, Innovation, Market, and Civic Engines
- Expand facilities and programming
- Pursue major federal funding (EDA, NSF, DoD, NASA)
- Strengthen employer recruitment and retention

**Key outcomes:**
- Fully integrated regional innovation ecosystem
- Strong federal and state funding pipeline
- Increased employer attraction and expansion
- Sustainable operating model with diversified revenue
- FSIC recognized as a regional anchor institution

**Facilities:** Positions FSIC as a permanent anchor institution for Southeast Volusia.

## SUSTAINABILITY

**Headline:** A Phased Model That Reduces Risk and Builds Momentum

**Body:**
FSIC launches with a lean operational model supported by philanthropic investment, in-kind contributions, and early public-private partnerships. Over three years, FSIC transitions from startup to full Talent Engine activation, supported by diversified revenue streams including grants, employer partnerships, earned revenue, and corporate sponsorships.

By Year 3, FSIC is positioned to sustain 30–50% of its operating budget through recurring earned revenue and employer partnerships, with grants and philanthropy fueling innovation and growth.
